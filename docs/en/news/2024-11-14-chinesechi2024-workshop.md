## Concurrent Workshops

## Conference Overview

Organized by International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI), Chinese CHI 2024 will take place **from November 22 to 25, 2024, at Southern University of Science and Technology in Shenzhen, China.** Its themes are Innovation, Creativity, and Integration.

Organized by International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI), this academic conference has become an important meeting point connecting Chinese HCI researchers, academia, and industry worldwide. Since 2012, Chinese CHI has been held successfully in Paris, Toronto, Seoul, Silicon Valley, Guangzhou, Montreal, Xiamen, and Bali, Indonesia. Conference papers are included in the ACM Digital Library and submitted for EI indexing.

In an era of emerging AI, the conference explores how technological innovation can transform interaction, stimulate creativity, shape more intelligent and human-centered interactive systems, and integrate multidisciplinary research to address complex real-world applications. We warmly invite leading scholars, researchers, and industry figures worldwide to share their latest findings and experience, exchange ideas, and explore diverse ways technology can empower humanity's future.

## Workshop 1

## A New Tool for Cross-Platform Interaction Innovation

Time: November 22, 9:00 am–12:00 pm

Venue: **Room 1002, Building C1, Nanshan Zhiyuan, Shenzhen**

Language: Chinese

Equipment Requirements: None

Using practical cases, this workshop introduces the research and applications of interaction design languages. Through the new IxDL card game and interactive demos, participants learn foundational concepts, broaden their understanding of interaction mechanisms, and reflect on interaction design's granularity. Groups then play two or three rounds of the IxDL game, exploring different approaches and insights, consolidating their understanding of new concepts and paradigms, and improving their perceptiveness, creativity, and efficiency in cross-platform interaction design.

### Target Audience:

- Interaction and experience designers, product managers, front-end developers, human–computer interaction researchers, and students

### Expected Outcomes:

- Discover new perspectives and ways of thinking about interaction design
- Learn new methods and tools for interaction-mechanism innovation through the IxDL card game
- Develop the ability to identify and create cross-platform interactions
- Improve communication within design teams and between designers and developers

## Workshop 2

Time: November 22, 9:00 am–12:00 pm

Venue: **Room 1108, Building C1, Nanshan Zhiyuan, Shenzhen**

Language: English/Chinese

Equipment: Laptop and Mobile Phone

Advances in science and technology have made the integration of biology and human–computer interaction (HCI) an emerging and promising research direction, creating biological human–computer interaction (Bio-HCI). Bio-HCI uses innovative biotechnology, biosensors, and biomaterials to redefine relationships among humans, living organisms, and computers through biodesign, investigating how these interactions can be supported and enhanced. This workshop offers an open discussion space to understand Bio-HCI's foundations, trends, and leading examples. Participants will apply these concepts in design exercises, generate interaction models, and discuss future applications and possibilities, encouraging innovation across fields.

### Target Audience:

- Design students, interaction designers, artists, and human–computer interaction (HCI) researchers interested in Bio-HCI
- Engineers and scientists interested in interdisciplinary research and innovation
- Students interested in Bio-HCI

### Expected Outcomes:

- Understand new research directions in human–computer interaction (HCI)
- Understand Bio-HCI trends, application methods, and tools and technologies, including sensors and IoT
- Strengthen interdisciplinary collaboration and communication in design teams
- Inspire broad reflection on future Bio-HCI innovations and applications
- Experiment with Bio-HCI design concepts

## Exploring Physiological Sensing as Embodied Cues to Enhance Co-Presence in the Metaverse

Time: November 22, 9:00 am–12:00 pm

Venue: **Room 1107, Building C1, Nanshan Zhiyuan, Shenzhen**

Language: English/Chinese

Equipment: Laptop or Tablet

This research uses psychophysiological information as embodied social cues to enhance large-scale co-presence in the metaverse, exploring people's perceptual preferences for different physiological data and representations. Each participant experiences a VR DJ performance incorporating five signals collected from real baseline users: electromyography (pEMG), galvanic skin response (GSR), heart rate (HR), respiration intensity (RE), and oxygen saturation (SpO2). After trying the prototype, participants complete an online survey about their preferences for the granularity and forms of physiological data representation, informing subsequent research and design decisions.

### Target Audience:

- Participants interested in human–computer interaction, VR devices, metaverse social interaction, physiological signal representation, and VR games are welcome.

### Expected Outcomes:

- Participants will learn about different physiological signals and their effects on physiological synchrony and perceived co-presence, and experience a metaverse environment integrating physiological data. Their feedback will inform future research and design.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2024-11-14-chinesechi2024-workshop-08.png)

## Workshop 6

**Future Archaeology: Experiencing, Discussing, and Testing Living Archives Through Hakka Culture**

Time: November 22, 9:00 am–12:00 pm

Venue: **Room 1012, Building C1, Nanshan Zhiyuan, Shenzhen**

Language: English/Chinese

Equipment: Participants Are Encouraged to Bring a Laptop

Shenzhen, a dense and dynamic city in southern China, exemplifies rapid urban transformation and development. Since its rise in the 1980s, rather than developing a collective memory, it has reflected the individual stories of people seeking opportunities from across the country.

Future Archaeology, a research project by the Materialities Research Group at Southern University of Science and Technology's School of Design, treats the city as a future archaeological site. It questions conventional historical reconstruction in our rapidly changing, connected society, combining community engagement and co-design with digital tools and AI to reconstruct both fact and fiction.

We propose a speculative Living Archive to challenge and reimagine the exploration and accessibility of cultural heritage, ultimately offering a participatory approach to urban archaeology. Engaging with tangible real materials and digital technologies, we question conventional narratives and reject singular perspectives. Using open-source APIs and web technologies, we develop a virtual realm connecting dispersed materials and stories, including regenerated data.

Focusing on Hakka culture in Shenzhen's Pingshan community, we seek to reveal hidden narratives and material dimensions through technological integration. Collaborative and speculative strategies connect history with present and future identities and actions, offering insights into interaction design, ethnography, applied computing, and human–computer interaction.

As part of the broader Future Archaeology project, we designed a web platform as a living archive of Hakka culture. It enables the public to explore a fictional historical space reconstructed from real remains, collected stories, community ideas, and AI tools generating text, 2D images, and 3D objects, through personalized rather than imposed narratives. Further development incorporates public comments, feedback, knowledge, and stories into cultural heritage that continually evolves and is never fixed.

### Target Audience:

- Researchers, practitioners, and students in design, media art, cultural heritage, social computing, AI, and technology, and anyone interested in material culture, urban studies, community participation, and narrative methods and practices

### Expected Outcomes:

- Explore and interact with the virtual space
- Contribute ideas and feedback to improve the participatory archive
- Experience being an active agent of cultural and material transformation
- Engage in creative conversations about the future of technology, cultural heritage, and materials
- Learn about Hakka culture

## Workshop 7

**Creating Creation: Building Multimodal Agents for AIGC with GenFlow**

Time: November 22, 9:00 am–12:00 pm

Venue: **Room 1003, Building C1, Nanshan Zhiyuan, Shenzhen**

Language: Chinese

Equipment: Laptop

As generative capabilities evolve and algorithms become products, generative AI agents have become essential creative tools. They generate images, video, speech, text, and other multimodal content, providing inspiration and practical outputs. Creation is a complex, multistage process involving repeated divergent and convergent thinking. Progress toward creative goals requires multiple rounds of iterative interaction with agents.

We propose GenFlow, a multimodal human–agent collaborative framework for complex creative tasks. By providing explainable, controllable interfaces in multiple modalities at different creative stages, it improves both experience and quality in tasks such as picture books, narrative comics, animated posters, and meeting records. The workshop introduces GenFlow and uses case studies and hands-on practice to explore, refine, and extend human–agent creative collaboration.

Hands-on activities comprise three design and research tasks: (1) using an agent-building platform and GenFlow to construct creative agents with multimodal AI capabilities for images, video, speech, and text; (2) producing high-quality creative work with those agents; and (3) cross-evaluating the results and discussing GenFlow's possibilities for multimodal human–agent collaboration.

The workshop offers scholars, practitioners, and enthusiasts in design, creativity, and interdisciplinary fields a useful reference and practical platform for design thinking and innovation in the GenAI era.

### Target Audience:

- Teachers and students with introductory experience in intelligent design, creative design, AIGC, AI, large models, or related fields are welcome, as are practitioners, independent creators, and creative media content producers.

### Expected Outcomes:

- Understand the concepts, principles, and architecture of GenFlow's human–agent creative collaboration framework
- Learn methods and tools for building agents that match users' creative needs and behavior, and create working examples
- Collect and integrate participants' discussions to guide further improvements to GenFlow and its practical value in multimodal human–AI collaboration, while encouraging deeper academic and industry research and discussion

## Workshop 6

**Future Archeology: Experiencing, Discussing, and Testing the Living Archive on the Example of Hakka Culture**

Date and Time: Oct. 22nd, 9:00 am -12:00 pm

Location: **Room 1012, Building C1,** iPark SUSTech School of Design

Language: English/Chinese

Equipment Requirement: Participants are encouraged to bring their own pc for a smooth and effective working session

Shenzhen, a high-density, dynamic city in southern China, showcases the swift transformation and developing character of urban areas. Since its emergence in the 1980s, it has not formed a collective memorybut rather reflects individual narratives from people across the country in search of opportunities.

The research project “Future Archaeology,” conducted by the Materialities Research Group at SUSTechSchool of Design, assumes the city is a future archaeological site. This raises questions about the effectiveness of conventional historical reconstruction methods in our rapidly changing, interconnected society. The research encompasses realistic and fictional reconstructions through digital tools and artificialintelligence based on community involvement and co-design.

We suggest a speculative archive, which we call “Living Archive,” to challenge and reimagine the investigation and accessibility of cultural heritage, ultimately contributing to a participatory approach to urbanarchaeology. We challenge conventional narratives and reject a singular viewpoint by engaging withtangible, authentic materials and digital technologies. We utilize open-source APIs and web technologies to develop a virtual realm that interlinks fragmented, collected materials and stories, encapsulatingregenerative data.

Focusing on the Hakka culture in Shenzhen’s Pingshan community, we aim to uncover hidden narrativesand material aspects through technological integration. We aim to offer new insights into interaction design, ethnographic studies, applied computing, and HCI, employing collaborative and speculative strategies to connect history with contemporary and future identities and actions.

As part of the broad project “Future Archeology,” we designed a web platform to become a Living Archive of Hakka culture where the public can explore material through a personal, non-imposed narrativenavigating a fictional historic space rebuilt with authentic remains, collected stories, and ideas from thecommunity, and AI tools of text, 2D images, and 3D objects generation. The further implementation ofthe archive includes integrating comments, feedback, knowledge and stories from the public to becomepart of the “never fixed,” evolving cultural heritage.

### Target Audience:

- Researchers, practitioners, and students in design, media art, cultural heritage, social computing, AIand technology, and anyone interested in material culture, urban studies, community engagement, andnarrative methods and practice.

### Participant Benefits:

- Navigate and interact with the virtual space;
- Contribute to the participative archive improvement with ideas and feedback;
Experience the feeling of being an active agent of a cultural and material transformation;
Engage in a creative conversation about the future of technology, cultural heritage, and materials;
- Learn about Hakka culture.

## Workshop 7

**Creation of Creations: Explainable and Controllable Interfaces in Human-Agent Co-Creation based GenFlow**

Date and Time: Oct. 22nd, 9:00 am -12:00 pm

Location: **Room 1003, Building C1,** iPark SUSTech School of Design

Language: Chinese

Equipment Requirement: Laptop

With the development and commercialization of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) technology, GenAI-based agents have been widely adopted in creative activities, offering diverse sources of inspiration results.Human-agent co-creation is a complex, multi-stage process in which each phase requires both divergent andconvergent thinking, gradually approaching the creative objectives through multiple rounds of interaction.However, a well-defined framework for user experience design in this process is currently lacking.

We proposed GenFlow, a mixed-initiative human-agent interaction framework designed for complex creativetasks. By emphasizing the provision of explainable and controllable interfaces for mixed-modal agents, thisframework enhances the creative experience and quality of complex tasks such as picture books, animatedposters, and meeting summaries. Built on the GenFlow, we designed an agent platform that integratesfoundational multimodal GenAI capabilities to support the creation and use of creative agents. By the designof controllable and interpretable user interfaces, the platform facilitates human-agent co-creation, enabling theproduction of higher-quality creative outcomes and improving overall system usability.
