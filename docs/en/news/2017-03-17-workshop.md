## Workshop 7: Collaborative Experience Innovation Through Service Design Tools

**Category:** Advanced workshop

### Speaker

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2017-03-17-workshop-02.png)

### Qingqing Cai

User experience strategist and specialist in user research and service design

More than eight years of user experience consulting experience

Tang Consulting

**Language:** Chinese

**Time:** Thursday, June 8, 2017, 15:00–18:00

### Background:

Service design requires substantial stakeholder engagement: customers who receive services, executives who coordinate resources, and staff who ultimately deliver them. Each role has different interests. Good service design must consider these interests and balance business requirements, customer needs, and staff efficiency. Service designers therefore coordinate stakeholders' interests and actively involve different roles in the design process. Service design tools are essential to those interactions.

### Course Outline:

- Introduction to service design processes and methods

- Service design tools

- Service design case practice

### Participant Benefits:

- Understand typical service design processes and methods

- Learn how to use tools to engage stakeholders in collaborative service design innovation

### Intended Audience:

- Executives, service designers, design strategists, marketers, service-sector entrepreneurs, project managers, customer relationship managers, and others interested in improving organizational, team, or personal working methods through service design methods and tools.

## Workshop 8: Making the Difficult Look Easy—Experience Design for Embedded Products

**Category:** Advanced workshop

### Speaker

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2017-03-17-workshop-03.png)

### Bei Zhang

Senior design leader, design consultant, and founder of a professional social networking product

Formerly at Tencent

**Language:** Chinese

**Time:** Thursday, June 8, 2017, 15:00–18:00

### Background:

H5 lightweight applications developed within WebView frameworks and many types of mini-programs have become common mobile products. Lightweight, installation-free, easy to share, quick to iterate, and small in storage footprint, they can fully meet users' contextual needs and even replace native applications. We call these embedded products.

Their experience hierarchy, information scale, and interface guidelines are strongly shaped or constrained by their host products and platforms. Balancing the scope of design while improving the host platform's experience requires skill. It is also worth examining how extending a platform's fundamental capabilities affects embedded products.

### Course Outline:

- Analyze differences between embedded and native product experiences, discussing design positioning, target users, drivers, methods, and principles

- Case analysis and problem-solving

- Design evaluation and interaction

### Participant Benefits:

- Design positioning and assessment methods

- Approaches and processes for developing design guidelines

- Applying user research methods in interaction design

### Intended Audience:

- Interaction designers, user researchers, design managers, and product planners

## Workshop 9: Optimizing Service Touchpoints

**Category:** Advanced workshop

### Speaker

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2017-03-17-workshop-04.png)

### Qizhang Chen

User Service Experience Director and Founder

Lingchao Creative Design

**Language:** Chinese

**Time:** Friday, June 9, 2017, 09:00–12:00

### Background:

User research has traditionally focused largely on electronic product use: services delivered through digital devices.

This workshop situates touchpoints within the service environment, allowing participants to experience what services might apply before a person uses a digital device. This helps future UI planning reflect users' real contexts more closely.

### Course Outline:

- What should researchers bring back from a service site?

- Identify touchpoints in field research data.

- Identify the activities performed at each touchpoint.

- Propose new interactions through group brainstorming.

- Optimize the service process.

### Participant Benefits:

- Introduction to service design.

- Understand how to deconstruct and apply service touchpoints.

- Exchange ideas with fellow participants.

- Understand how to run an effective workshop.

### Intended Audience:

- UX and IxD researchers and practitioners interested in expanding into service design.

- Managers and business owners planning to introduce service design next year.

## Workshop 10: Design Relativity—Designing Payments After the Red Envelope Era

**Category:** Advanced workshop

### Speaker

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2017-03-17-workshop-05.png)

### Yimin Mo

Visual Design Lead, Tencent FiT Financial Marketing Department

Tencent

**Language:** Chinese

**Time:** Friday, June 9, 2017, 09:00–12:00

### Background:

What is Internet finance? Tencent has moved from digital red envelopes to payments and then to a broader Internet finance ecosystem. As user groups diversify and products proliferate, designers must create products with different personalities within one system. Beginning with red envelopes, we developed integrated, phone-based payments around everyday needs, then used Internet thinking and user understanding to encourage traditional businesses to embrace Internet-enabled payment. We next built payment networks and deeper financial networks. How can we introduce users to this serious world in an approachable way, beginning with personal money management and helping the wider public understand finance and participate in accessible, inclusive financial experiences?

The workshop examines design during Tencent's transition from red envelopes to payments and Internet finance, in an era dominated by content-focused flat design. How can asking and answering questions improve design thinking? How can UI creativity survive growing reliance on flat styles, standards, and templates, avoiding identical product characteristics and personalities? Participants experience how small design elements can convey care for users and a product's personality within a serious financial context. We also consider how empathy can prevent friction between product needs and the differentiated designs arising from these characteristics.

### Course Outline:

- Tencent's Approach to Internet Finance

- Designing payments after the red envelope era

- From a single-skill designer to a solution-oriented designer

### Participant Benefits:

- Understand Tencent's evolution from digital red envelopes to payments and Internet finance.

- Learn through cases how to apply design thinking to design.

- Experience the shift from designer-centered thinking to practical thinking integrating users, products, and design.

- Understand how we set design goals, achieve outcomes, and think holistically to develop solution-oriented designers.

### Intended Audience:

- Product managers, designers, design managers, and people who find design increasingly difficult to understand as they practice it.

## Workshop 11: A New Dimension of Interaction—Dynamic Interaction Chains

**Category:** Advanced workshop

### Speaker

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2017-03-17-workshop-06.png)

### Bin Zhu

**Language:** Chinese

**Time:** Friday, June 9, 2017, 15:00–18:00

## Course Abstract:

Traditional interaction design has successfully used two-dimensional prototypes, controls, and logical relationships for a decade. As software and hardware advance, higher-dimensional motion design is showing compelling advantages, producing a bewildering variety of effects and elaborate solutions. We experienced this phase too, but realized that we were merely using higher-dimensional techniques in a lower-dimensional way, rather than reconstructing and improving the entire system from a higher-dimensional perspective. Motion design should not simply evolve visual effects; it should carry the complete user journey. Creating an end-to-end motion experience throughout a system opens a new realm of interaction.

### Course Outline:

- Motion design cases and practical lessons and experience

- Group simulations of end-to-end motion design scenarios and paper models

- Sharing, discussion, and Q&A

### Participant Benefits:

- Understand the difference between isolated animation effects and system-level animation design

- Try constructing a system-level animation model

- Distinguish and align system animation with interaction logic

- Explore how to move beyond a small screen to create larger interaction spaces

### Intended Audience:

- UI、GUI designer

- User experience designers

- Product managers

- People interested in animation design and interaction
