The 12th International Chinese human–computer interaction Conference, jointly organized by International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI) and Southern University of Science and Technology, was held successfully at Southern University of Science and Technology in Shenzhen from November 22 to 25, 2024. Under the theme Innovation, Creativity, and Integration, it brought together over 500 experts and scholars worldwide to explore frontier developments in human–computer interaction.

The Chinese CHI 2024 Digital Art Exhibition was a major highlight, expressing the conference theme through interdisciplinary innovation. Its four sections—Enhancing Lives, Blurring Boundaries, Affective Interfaces, and Cultural Fusion—encouraged creators to cross traditional boundaries, combine innovative thinking and technology, and explore unconventional human–computer interaction.

### EMBERS - Improve survival chances beyond critical 72-hour period for those trapped in rubble from conflicts and disasters by maintaining a positive mental attitude through RF communication and HCI method

**Authors:**

Mengze Zou\
Imperial College London & Royal College of Art\
Zhejun Zhang\
China Academy of Art\
Xinhang Guo\
The University of Manchester\
Gareth Loudon\
Supervisor, Royal College of Art\
Stephen Green\
Supervisor, Imperial College London

**Description:**

EMBERS establishes two-way communication to sustain the will to survive among people trapped in rubble until rescuers arrive. It also lets rescue teams locate them using inexpensive equipment, particularly important where ground-penetrating radar is unavailable. Compact hardware comprises a microcontroller, low-power RF transceiver, vibration motor, piezoelectric ceramic, and battery, activated manually or through external pressure. Research suggests vibrations mimicking deep breathing can unconsciously encourage slower breathing, reducing anxiety and panic. Activation triggers vibration and a signal that remotely activates nearby EMBERS devices. Their continuing vibrations reassure trapped people that they are not alone. Remote activation keeps devices useful when users cannot move their hands. Rescuers can also activate them on the same frequency; preset messages switch to rapid vibrations to signal that help is nearby and sustain hope.

EMBERS helps those trapped under rubble maintain their will to survive by establishing two-way communication until rescue arrives. It also enables rescue teams to locate them using low-cost devices, crucial in less developed areas lacking penetrating radar. The EMBERS hardware consists of a microcontroller, low-power RF transceiver, vibration motor, piezoelectric ceramic, and battery, integrated into a compact size. The device can be activated by manual or external presses. Research shows that vibrations mimicking deep breathing rates can subconsciously induce deep breathing, helping the trapped individuals reduce anxiety and panic. When they activate their device, it vibrates and sends a signal to remotely activate other nearby EMBERS devices. Continuous vibrations from each other's devices reduce loneliness by making trapped individuals aware they are not alone. If a person's hands are restricted, remote activation ensures the device still works. Rescue personnel can also remotely activate devices on the same radio frequency. Different preset information allows the device to switch to a rapid vibration mode, signalling the trapped person that the rescue team is nearby, helping maintain their will to survive.
