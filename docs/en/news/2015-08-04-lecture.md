On July 29, Professor Xiangshi Ren of Kochi University of Technology delivered an HCI lecture at University of Science and Technology Beijing entitled ***Past, Present, Future***. It covered **an overview of human–computer interaction** and **methods for writing research papers**.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2015-08-04-lecture-01.jpg)

Professor Ren began with the history of human–computer interaction. As human–computer interaction does not yet have a universally accepted historical account, he presented his own summary of the history of human–computer interaction:

The invention of the graphical user interface at Xerox PARC opened a chapter in human–computer interaction history. The lecture discussed AI pioneer J. C. R. Licklider, mouse inventor Douglas C. Engelbart, information-age visionary Vannevar Bush, and CMU polymath Herbert A. Simon. Cybernetics founder Norbert Wiener also played an important role in HCI's development.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2015-08-04-lecture-02.png)

A handwritten note from mouse inventor Engelbart to Professor Xiangshi Ren (2006): "Let's focus our HCI attention on increasing human capabilities to develop, integrate and understand the knowledge required for improving society's survival probability."

ACM CHI is HCI's leading conference. Since its establishment in 1982, it has attracted researchers worldwide and become ACM's second-largest conference after computer graphics. A US strategic report in 2001 identified HCI as a key future technology, while the ACM Classification System likewise recognizes it as a top-level field.

Today's human–computer interaction still involves unbalanced communication between people and machines, but we are gradually moving from micro-level to macro-level HCI. human–computer interaction is developing rapidly. VR and AR are gaining prominence, while AI has become a major focus across computing. Some predict that by 2050 humanity will enter a Super AI era, in which people work under computers' guidance.

Professor Xiangshi Ren has his own view of the future of human–computer interaction. Inspired by philosophy, he proposes HEC (Human-Engaged Computing). Unlike the Super AI vision, his approach favors people directing computers and working with them synergistically to address global problems.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2015-08-04-lecture-03.png)

The lecture included an engaging meditation session. Professor Ren asked everyone to sit quietly with their eyes closed while he played the sound of ocean waves. He explained that meditation can change brain structure and described its alpha waves as having a frequency close to cosmic waves. Smiling, he called meditation "a dialogue between humanity and the universe."

After this relaxing meditation, Professor Xiangshi Ren continued with advice on writing high-quality papers.

He outlined a paper's structure, illustrated in the figure, comparing its shape to an elegant vase. He also emphasized integrating theory and practice, and uniting knowledge with action.
