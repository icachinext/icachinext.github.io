## ICACHI Young Scholars Committee

## Yuhao Sun

Yuhao Sun received a master's degree from Imperial College London's Hamlyn Centre in 2021 and is now a doctoral student in the University of Edinburgh's medical school. Research interests include biomedical informatics, human–computer interaction, and other computing-assisted healthcare technologies.

## The Hamlyn Centre, Imperial College London

**Imperial College London's Hamlyn Centre develops safe, effective, and accessible technologies to improve healthcare in developing and developed countries.** It **focuses on technological innovation and emphasizes translation from laboratory to clinical practice.** Part of the Institute of Global Health Innovation, the center receives generous philanthropic support from the Helen Hamlyn Trust and Lady Hamlyn.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2021-12-04-article-rdu0qvjv-02.jpg)

**In healthcare, the Hamlyn Centre works at the frontiers of imaging, sensing, and robotics to address global health challenges linked to demographic, environmental, social, and economic change.** These areas include:

## 1. Imaging

Cardiovascular imaging, image-guided intervention, surgical imaging and vision, surgical imaging technologies, and biophotonics.

## 2. Sensing

Body sensor networks, surgical implants, IoT and intelligent environments, and ESPRIT.

## 3. Robotics

Surgical robotics, assistive robotics, micro- and nanorobotics, and brain–computer interfaces.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2021-12-04-article-rdu0qvjv-03.jpg)

The Hamlyn Centre holds several annual events. **The Hamlyn Symposium on Medical Robotics began in 2007.** Attendance grew from approximately 50 delegates in 2007 to 600 in 2019. **It has become an annual international forum where clinicians, engineers, and researchers exchange ideas and explore healthcare technology's emerging challenges and opportunities.**

The annual **Hamlyn Winter School on Surgical Imaging and Vision focuses on technical and clinical aspects of surgical imaging and vision.** Through lectures, workshops, and small projects, it introduces researchers to frontier work in this rapidly advancing field. Topics include fundamentals and state-of-the-art surgical imaging, vision algorithms for tracking, 3D scene reconstruction and navigation, intraoperative registration and relocalization, and inverse-realism-based multimodal image fusion and real-time augmented reality systems.
