## ICACHI Young Scholars Committee

### Chen Wang

Chen Wang is a doctoral student at Japan's Kochi University of Technology, supervised by Professor Xiangshi Ren, researching Human-Engaged Computing and computational aesthetics.

## Human-Engaged Computing Institute: CHEC

**The Center for Human-Engaged Computing (CHEC), also known as the Human-Engaged or Human-Harmonized Computing institute, was founded in 2015 at Japan's Kochi University of Technology.** Its predecessors were the Center for Human-Computer Interaction established in 2012 at Kochi University of Technology, specializing in human–computer interaction technologies, and the human–computer interaction Laboratory (Ren Lab), founded in 2000. **Its founder and director is Professor Xiangshi Ren (REN Xiangshi),** founding and lifetime honorary president of International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI), foreign member of the Engineering Academy of Japan, and fellow of the Information Processing Society of Japan.

Professor Xiangshi Ren has conducted fundamental and applied human–computer interaction research since 1991. Drawing on three decades of experience, CHEC studies interactions between people and computing devices and how technology shapes human factors. **Its work covers diverse human–computer interaction topics, including pen interaction, input modeling, adaptive interfaces for disadvantaged users, and haptic devices.** Two papers in the leading human–computer interaction journal ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction—Improving Selection Performance on Pen-Based Systems: A Study of Pen-Based Interaction for Selection Tasks (2000), and Differences and Similarities between Finger and Pen Stroke Gestures on Stationary and Mobile Devices (2015)—advanced pen and touch research on stationary and mobile devices. CHEC has published more than 300 papers at leading human–computer interaction conferences including ACM CHI, UIST, ITS, DIS, and IFIP INTERACT, and journals including TOCHI, IJHCS, IJHCI, and BIT.

**In 2006, Professor Xiangshi Ren visited computing pioneer Douglas Engelbart in Silicon Valley.** Engelbart shared: "Let's focus our HCI attention on increasing human capabilities to develop, integrate and understand the knowledge required for improving society's survival probability"—a call for human–computer interaction to strengthen human capabilities. **This inspired Professor Xiangshi Ren and CHEC to reconsider human–computer interaction's fundamental contribution.** Traditional human–computer interaction still lacked clear long-term goals, while evaluation remained confined to industrial-era performance criteria such as usability, efficiency, and naturalness. After sustained reflection and experience, Professor Xiangshi Ren proposed Human-Engaged Computing (Human-Engaged Computing, HEC), encouraging the community to rethink future human–computer relations.

**HEC places human factors at the center of human–computer interaction, AI, and all technological development and outcomes. Human and technological capabilities should advance together, maximizing mutual improvement and synergism while minimizing antibiosis. The goal is to continually enhance human wisdom and unlock full potential to solve complex real-world problems and improve humanity's prospects of survival.** HEC encourages human–computer interaction researchers and designers to consider technology's adverse effects while creating it, recognize and explore human value, and broaden current human–computer interaction perspectives and paradigms. **For more on HEC theory, see:**

**[https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/5Fh68A76rLkAwFPSnqdlhA](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/5Fh68A76rLkAwFPSnqdlhA)**

**CHEC's work now has two main areas. The first develops HEC theory and explores HCI's and HEC's meaning from multiple perspectives.** Specific topics include the HEC framework and technological support for soft human capabilities such as mindfulness, aesthetics, and cognition. **The second continues fundamental interaction research, with highly regarded work on hand–eye coordination modeling and design guidance for mobile input and input correction strategies.** Its implementations span software and hardware, drawing on philosophy, psychology, cognitive science, neuroscience, and sensing. CHEC benefits from the support of experts, researchers, administrators, and students worldwide and maintains close collaborations with universities and research organizations in China and abroad.

**CHEC's members come from many countries, bringing multicultural creativity and possibilities** and building a bridge of friendship between East and West. We live, learn, work, and share together; graduates continue pursuing our shared vision worldwide. **We look forward to welcoming more creative colleagues to CHEC.** Through our work, we hope to help young scholars grasp human–computer interaction's broader direction and inspire its next wave. We welcome scholars and students to develop HEC thought through new perspectives, research, and practice, recognize its potential to guide future design, and advance human–computer interaction's contributions to society.
