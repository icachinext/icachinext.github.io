Session 1 of the Human-Engaged Computing and Technology for Good online forum took place on May 23 Beijing Time, simulcast by Tencent Wiz and XuetangX. Organized by International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI), the series showcases Chinese scholars' shared commitment to combating the pandemic through academic work. As society and the economy slowly restart after the global pandemic's pause, we seek to confront challenges facing humanity and reconsider technology's role in society. We aim to envision more forward-looking, resilient, and inclusive human–computer interaction models, develop solutions for known and unknown problems, inspire reflection on progress, and strengthen our capacity to respond to crises.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-03-recap-forum-02.jpg)

The session was moderated by Xiangshi Ren of Japan's Kochi University of Technology. At the opening, Xiangshi Ren introduced International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction. International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction is the first international professional scientific association founded by Chinese scholars worldwide. International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction seeks to use its resources and global networks in human–computer interaction, AI, user experience, media and design, and humanities to build an open international scientific platform. It contributes to technology-for-good research and practice oriented toward humanity's future and scientific development.

### Xiangshi Ren

Kochi University of Technology, Japan

## Presentation Topic

Paradigm Shift from Human-Computer Interaction to Human-Engaged Computing

This session addressed Human-Engaged Computing and how human–computer relations should be reconsidered for humanity's future. Xiangshi Ren discussed the fundamental ideas of Human-Engaged Computing. Inspired by a message from human–computer interaction pioneer Douglas Carl Engelbart, Xiangshi Ren developed the concept of Human-Engaged Computing through study and investigation.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-03-recap-forum-05.jpg)

Xiangshi Ren discussed the fundamental ideas of Human-Engaged Computing. Inspired by a message from human–computer interaction pioneer Douglas Carl Engelbart, Xiangshi Ren developed Human-Engaged Computing through study and investigation. Closely related theories include Joseph Licklider's human–computer symbiosis from the 1960s and Umer Farooq and Jonathan's human–computer integration from the 2010s. Human–computer synergy emphasizes mutual advancement and improvement. Within this framework, Xiangshi Ren also proposed human–computer antagonism. As a guiding framework, Human-Engaged Computing informs Human-Engaged Computing's argument that future computing should develop human mental capabilities and help people explore their potential. human–computer interaction should become a pathway or environment for capability development rather than merely input and output.

### Zhiyong Fu

Tsinghua University

## Presentation Topic

Empathy and Symbiosis: Design preferable future AI product and service

Zhiyong Fu spoke about designing more desirable future AI products and services. AI applications in human–computer interaction and design have renewed debate about human–machine relations. As AI becomes increasingly powerful, researchers and practitioners must reconsider the harmonious coexistence of technology, society, and culture. Future AI product and service design will reshape relationships among people, communities, and environments, while its application in human–computer interaction transforms design processes and methods. Designers and researchers need an empathetic reassessment of ethical and emotional factors in future AI scenarios. AI will become a design partner, contributing new methods and tools that complement the strengths of minds and machines to create a better future. Human–computer synergy will provide important technical support for future human–computer interaction and design research, enabling human and technological capabilities to develop together organically.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-03-recap-forum-08.png)

Technological development also transforms social life and values. Relationships among society, technology, and culture have become a research focus, prompting methods such as critical design, speculative design, and design fiction to redirect design. Designs informed by future technological trends can reveal possible ways of living and new human–computer interaction models. Addressing the challenge of designing futures, Professor Fu discussed embodied and symbiotic tools: seeking solutions to present problems within future scenarios, then bringing those solutions back to reality to create a better future.

### Jianmin Wang

Tongji University

## Presentation Topic

Make Automated Drive Acting like a Man based on the Collaborative Driving Synergized Interaction

Jianmin Wang focused on intelligent vehicles and reflected on human–machine relations. In the AI era, different degrees of overlap between human and machine intelligence reflect different levels of participation in synergy. Today's discussion must address intelligence itself in depth: computers are no longer merely passive receivers. Human cognition, environments, society, and culture must also be considered.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-03-recap-forum-11.jpg)

Building on this theoretical background, Jianmin Wang discussed interaction design for intelligent connected vehicles. Research into vehicle human–computer interaction encompasses five dimensions, integrating people, vehicles, connected vehicles and 5G, IoT, and the Internet. Driver safety is central to human–computer interaction design and improved vehicle experiences. Research moves from individual devices and multi-device collaboration to holistic human–computer interaction across people, vehicles, environments, and society. AI is already widely used in vehicles; as cars perceive, judge, and decide, their relationship with people will be redefined. Collaboration between people and intelligent vehicles will be another important topic.

### Xiaojuan Ma

The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology

## Presentation Topic

Manifesting AI Engagement in Human-Engaged Computing

Xiaojuan Ma emphasized collaboration between AI and people. Human–computer synergy ultimately seeks improvement on both sides. In Human-Engaged AI, important questions include how to achieve engaged humans and engaging AI, and how to design AI behavior based on both.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-03-recap-forum-14.png)

The research distinguishes four dimensions of human engagement: attentional, emotional, cognitive, and behavioral. Designing AI behavior to achieve engaging AI is a central concern. Current design considers four aspects: state of process, usage of resources, degree of autonomy, and level of performance. Xiaojuan Ma explained these dimensions through practical examples.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-03-recap-forum-16.png)

### Xiaojun Yuan

State University of New York, USA

## University at Albany

## Presentation Topic

Voice Search and Human-Engaged Computing

Xiaojun Yuan discussed applying human–computer synergy to voice search. Xiaojun Yuan presented a questionnaire study analyzing voice-search users' habits. Users interacted relatively little with voice systems, and spoken queries were longer than typed ones, consistent with earlier findings. The study also found that more user training involving intentional words—such as why, when, and where—could substantially improve search results, interaction, and system learning.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-03-recap-forum-17.png)

Drawing on several studies, Xiaojun Yuan also discussed challenges in voice search. One possible response is to apply human–computer synergy: creating dialogue between users and systems so that they assist one another and improve both sides' capabilities.
