## Lab Tour

Lab Tour is a series on ICACHI's WeChat platform introducing laboratories and frontier research directions in human–computer interaction, broadening the perspectives of human–computer interaction researchers.

As computing permeates every corner of life, we have never depended so heavily on the digital world. Its opportunities and challenges are evident. As a bridge between people and machines, human–computer interaction has become a major focus of research institutions and businesses over the past 30 years.

In October 2012, Kochi University of Technology in Japan established CHCI, later renamed CHEC, under Professor Xiangshi Ren. The laboratory develops next-generation interaction technologies that support collaborative problem-solving between people and computers. Based in Asia with a global outlook, it draws on Eastern and Western cultures and connects their research communities. Its students and scholars also come from varied disciplines, bringing broad knowledge and perspectives to their work.

## Science

The laboratory's main research areas are **Game Interaction**, **User Interface Design for the Ageing Population**, **Assistive Interfaces for Blind People**, **Haptic Interfaces**, and the emerging topic of **Self-regulated Learning in Virtual Environments**.

In the editor's view, body gestures and haptic interfaces are central to the laboratory's projects, reflecting broader research interests and trends in human–computer interaction.

Nintendo Wii pioneered commercial gesture-based gaming, and Microsoft Kinect subsequently took it further. Although their operating principles differ, both involve body gestures from the user's perspective. Gesture interaction will likely find applications across human–computer interaction far beyond gaming, making its research value clear.

The editor also sees enormous potential in haptic interfaces. People obtain information from the environment through hearing, touch, and vision. Earlier research often assumed sight and hearing as defaults and has explored them extensively. Most people routinely use those senses to interact with their surroundings, but touch is particularly important for blind and older people, making appropriate haptic interfaces essential. Meanwhile, rapid advances in electronics are introducing new environments such as virtual reality, where touch offers a controllable and effective way for machines to provide feedback.

## Philosophy

The name CHEC (Human-Engaged Computing) expresses the laboratory's aim to address human–computer interaction through synergized interaction between humans and computers.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2015-07-12-article-uiuouzsc-01.png)

Figure 1. Human-Engaged Computing framework (source: www.xrenlab.com)

CHEC's name embodies a philosophical outlook. Frontier science is not always distinguished by technological complexity, but by the thinking it expresses. This is one reason to pursue a Doctor of Philosophy: to advance both technical skills and philosophical understanding.

Among CHEC's recent publications, the CHI 2015 paper Leveraging and Integrating Eastern and Western Insights for Human Engagement Studies in HCI caught the editor's attention. It repeatedly discusses the I Ching, a foundation of natural philosophy and human practice in traditional Chinese thought. Its holistic perspective sees the world through universal connections, mutual influence, interpenetration, and constraint. This accords with multidimensional thinking in modern philosophy, which likewise emphasizes interrelationships.

The paper focuses on human engagement and East–West integration. The first part relates engagement factors to sensory experience, emotion, and needs. The second emphasizes the holistic outlook of the I Ching and seeks to combine it with Western analytical approaches to better meet human needs. It then discusses the importance of integrating Eastern and Western insights into human–computer interaction research through theory, practice, reflection, and process.

CHEC brings science and philosophy together in its research practice, helping it reach new heights. Readers can learn more through [http://www.xrenlab.com/](http://www.xrenlab.com/)，.
