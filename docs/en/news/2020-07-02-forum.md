## ICACHI Online Forum

As society and the economy slowly restart after the global pandemic's pause, we seek to confront humanity's future challenges and reconsider technology's role in society. We aim to envision more forward-looking, resilient, and inclusive human–computer interaction models, develop applications and solutions for known and unknown problems, inspire reflection on progress, and strengthen crisis response.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-07-02-forum-01.jpg)

**Session 4: Designing Futures—Methods, Tools, and Practice**

In a rapidly changing world, exploring future trends, challenges, and opportunities and creating future value through design is a new challenge.

Moving from object-, user-, and society-centered approaches to future-centered design, this salon brings the future development of all subjects and objects—including humanity—into design's scope and criteria. It introduces futures thinking into design thought and practice. Grounded in design and futures studies, designing futures adds reflection on worldviews and values and helps creators examine present design through possible futures. We also introduce speculative and critical perspectives, discussing concepts, methods, tools, and cases through education and practice. We hope to explore future possibilities through design, inspire more designers to participate, and create desirable futures together.

## Presentation by Professor Zhiyong Fu

**Topic:** Envisioning Future Scenarios—Integrating Futures Studies into Interaction Design Education and Research

**Abstract:** Integrating futures research with design practice is a focus of the emerging field of designing futures. Futures studies, or futurology, comprehensively examines trends, possible scenarios, challenges, and responses in major areas of human life. Compared with design, it addresses larger, more complex world systems. Incorporating its methods and tools into design processes helps designers consider long-term visions and value. This talk shares course practices integrating interaction design and futures studies at Tsinghua University's Academy of Arts and Design and introduces tools developed by the Tsinghua team. It explores ways to integrate futures thinking and values into design, enhancing innovation and broadening design's impact.

## Presentation by Professor Peter Scupelli

**Topic:** Dexign Futures for Challenging Times

**Abstract:** We live amid challenging times: the global COVID-19 pandemic, the ongoing climate emergency, and, most recently, global demonstrations in solidarity with the Black Lives Matter movement in the United States. How should designers respond in such confusing times? These three problems require new ways of thinking that align short-term design action with long-term visions. Different design problems demand new methods. This talk focuses on an experimental approach combining design thinking with futures thinking, named Dexign Futures by design master Arnold Wasserman. Professor Scupelli co-taught the first Dexign Futures class with Wasserman at Carnegie Mellon University in 2013. He subsequently developed it into a flipped-classroom course now taught to all students in Carnegie Mellon's School of Design. This lecture describes the Dexign Futures open-source project.

## Presentation by Professor Li Zhang

**Topic:** Discursive Futures and Alternative Things

**Abstract:** Design's interest in the future arises both from pressure on the discipline to evolve and from design's inherently future-oriented nature. Starting with the concept of things, this lecture presents discursive futures based on alternative things. Alternatives are central to generating meaning, and things have always profoundly shaped everyday life. The history and trajectory of designing things show their meaning increasingly entering language and acquiring value, particularly in today's Internet and social media contexts. As things move from products emphasizing functional mechanisms to language emphasizing constructed uses, their alternative possibilities provide a logic for defining the direction and forms of designing futures. Discursive futures offer desirable possibilities centered on communication ethics.
