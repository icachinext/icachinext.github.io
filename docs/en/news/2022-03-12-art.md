## ICACHI Young Scholars Committee

## Yuqi Liu

Yuqi Liu received a PhD from Kyushu University's Graduate School of Design in 2021. A China Scholarship Council scholarship recipient and outstanding doctoral graduate, she is a member of the Japanese Society for the Science of Design and Japan Society of Kansei Engineering. She was a research assistant at Kyushu and a short-term exchange student in design thinking and social innovation at Stanford. Now a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Information Art and Design at Tsinghua University's Academy of Arts and Design, she studies design futures and philosophy, design and futures thinking, aging societies and digital well-being, age-friendly products and services, SDGs, and sustainable societies. She has presented at IASDR, KEER, HCII, and ICCIA, received a KEER 2020 Best Paper Award, and published 16 papers in SCI-, SSCI-, and EI-indexed venues and leading Chinese and national journals.

## Kyushu University's Graduate School of Design, Japan

**Kyushu University**, known as Kyudai in Japanese, **was founded in 1903. It is a leading national comprehensive research university, one of Japan's seven former imperial universities and a top-tier Type A institution in the Top Global University Project.** It belongs to the engineering federation of eight universities, the Research University 11 consortium, and the designated national universities, and participates in the UK–Japan RENKEI and Sweden–Japan Mirai programs. **It ranked fourth in Japan in THE's 2021 rankings and 137th worldwide in QS 2022.**

## Kyushu University's Graduate School of Design, Japan

Japanese design institutions include national/public and private universities. **Kyushu is one of the few national/public universities offering design programs.** Other notable public institutions include Chiba and Tsukuba, while private art universities include Musashino and Tama Art University.

**Kyushu's design school was established in 1968. Its undergraduate school currently offers five specializations:**

- Environmental Design
- Industrial Design
- Design Futures
- Media Design
- Acoustic Design

**The graduate school offers six research specializations:**

- Human Science in Design
- Human Science in Design (International)
- Communication Design Science
- Environmental and Heritage Design
- Content and Creative Design
- Design Strategy

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2022-03-12-art-05.png)

Kyushu's design school adopts Design the Future of Design as its vision. Among its five undergraduate areas, Design Futures now serves as a coordinating core for environmental, industrial, media, and acoustic design, as illustrated below. Preserving traditional strengths while entering future-oriented fields, the school develops highly creative professionals with international perspectives and capabilities for the 21st century. It encourages interdisciplinary learning, integrated perspectives, exploration, and new challenges, preparing advanced designers who create new value. Design engineering combines engineering as technology's practical foundation with art as free expression of the human spirit. It guides technological development and studies science and technology's social applications through both fields' integrated ethos, seeking to humanize technology. It trains designers spanning humanities, social sciences, and natural sciences, with engineering knowledge, insight into people and society, and creative artistic sensitivity.

## Design Futures at Kyushu University's Graduate School of Design

Reflecting the research interests of Tsinghua University's Design Futures Laboratory, the following introduces Kyushu's new Design Futures specialization, launched in April 2020.

**In an era of change, society's expectations of designers' imagination and creativity are higher than ever. We seek a diverse, sustainably flourishing society of coexistence. Design Futures focuses on three areas essential to society's future: Art and Design, Social Futures, and Life and Information Sciences.**

## Tamura Laboratory, Kyushu University Graduate School of Design

**Ryoichi Tamura is an associate professor of design strategy at Kyushu University; visiting scholar at Illinois Institute of Technology's Institute of Design; board member and fifth-branch head of the Japanese Society for the Science of Design; session chair in the Japan Society of Kansei Engineering; deputy director of Kyushu's Center for Designed Futures; and member of the design school's reform implementation committee.**

Professor Tamura argues that **design's effectiveness as a management resource depends not only on coordinating people, materials, and money, but also on information and time.** Tangible and intangible aspects of societies, organizations, and businesses are intricately linked with design. Growing complexity and uncertainty make ideal solutions difficult to find. **Designers must analyze specific social circumstances and systematically examine relationships among designed things and experiences, producers, and users, including the combined relationships linking all three.**

The laboratory studies desirable design concepts and development processes. **To improve quality of life, its recent work addresses services in light of expanding design targets,** older adults in light of changing users, and energy in light of changing social environments.

## 1. Health, Well-Being, and Care for Older Adults

Building on Japan's integrated community care system for health, medical, and long-term care services, Kyushu University, Itoshima City, and Sumitomo Riko established a partnership and the Kyushu University Healthcare System LABO Itoshima, commonly Fureai Lab, at Itoshima's Fureai Health and Welfare Center. Working with Fureai Lab, the group studies service design using everyday information to improve healthy older adults' quality of life. Intelligent IoT welfare devices—including pressure sensors, motion-based games, smart beds, and watches—collect daily living and behavioral data. Quantitative analysis and visualization reveal design approaches for improving quality of life.

## 2. Kansei Value and Product/Service Design

Professor Tamura teaches kansei value cognition, design management, design systems, service design, industrial design, and strategic architecture. The laboratory consequently explores products and services through kansei value, guided by corporate strategy and customer satisfaction and aimed at improving quality of life. Examples include mobility products and services studied from a kansei-value perspective.

## 3. Sustainability and Social System Design

Professor Tamura focuses on applying design to social problems. The laboratory studies sustainable social systems, including energy systems using smart meters. Starting with quality of life and behavioral change, this research explores how design can encourage changes in everyday habits, improve well-being and energy efficiency, reduce consumption, and enable low-carbon lifestyles and residential communities.
