**@Science & Technology Review**

Authors: Chen Wang and Xiangshi Ren

As an interdisciplinary field, human–computer interaction has become an important driver of the information age and of bringing computing technologies to the public. While reviewing current developments in human–computer interaction, this article traces the evolution of the ideas behind technology and develops a broader philosophical understanding of human–computer relationships and future innovation. It outlines the position and contributions of human–computer interaction, examines technological developments through the history of human–computer interaction, and introduces the theory of Human-Engaged Computing by considering tensions between people and computers and the ideal relationship between humans and technology. It then offers recommendations in five areas—academic thought, research and education, societal impact, communication, and product technologies—to inspire future directions for human–computer interaction and related information technologies.
