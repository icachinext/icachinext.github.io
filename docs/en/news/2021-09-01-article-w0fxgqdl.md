## UCL Interaction Centre (UCLIC)

**The UCL Interaction Centre (UCLIC, pronounced you-click; [https://uclic.ucl.ac.uk](https://uclic.ucl.ac.uk)) is jointly affiliated with UCL's Department of Computer Science in the Faculty of Engineering and Department of Psychology in the Faculty of Brain Sciences. A world-leading human–computer interaction research and teaching institution, UCLIC has more than 60 years of human–computer interaction history, originating in UCL's Ergonomics Unit around 1960.**

**UCLIC's current director is Professor Yvonne Rogers, a pioneering human–computer interaction researcher** whose nearly 40 years of work have advanced human–computer interaction. Her Interaction Design: Beyond Human-Computer Interaction is a classic international human–computer interaction textbook. Deputy director Professor Nadia Berthouze was among the earliest pioneers of affective computing and interaction. **Her extensive research on bodily movement as a modality for affective computing and human–computer interaction design expanded a field long dominated by vision, hearing, and text.**

**One of the world's largest human–computer interaction groups, UCLIC has 16 professors and faculty members, 55 researchers and doctoral students, and 33 visiting experts and scholars worldwide.** Its distinguished scientists include interaction design pioneer Yvonne Rogers, digital health researcher Ann Blandford, affective computing researcher Nadia Berthouze, multisensory experience researcher Mariana Obrist, and disability and inclusive design researcher Catherine Holloway. **UCLIC also co-founded the Global Disability Innovation Hub (GDI Hub; [https://www.disabilityinnovation.com](https://www.disabilityinnovation.com)）), conducting international disability and inclusive design research and projects and becoming WHO's first collaborating research unit in this area.**

**UCLIC covers numerous human–computer interaction topics: human-centered AI, real-world digital healthcare, affective computing and interaction, health and well-being, usability and disability-enabling technologies, collaboration and communication, real-world interaction, cognition and attention, multimodal sensing, physiological computing, data visualization, future interfaces, physical computing, educational technology, persuasive interaction, AI and human–computer interaction, touch, taste and smell technologies, metamaterials, and levitation displays.**
