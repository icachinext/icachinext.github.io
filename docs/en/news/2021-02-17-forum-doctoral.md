## About the Forum

Humanity, Technology, and the Future: Reflections on the Post-AI Era is jointly initiated by the Young Scholars Committee of International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI) and the Innovation Promotion Committee of the Chinese Professors Association in Japan. It examines humanity and technology over the long term and opens discussion of the post-AI era.

**The forum will take place online on the evening of March 21, 2021, Beijing Time. Outstanding doctoral students are invited to present and discuss their work:** introduce their research and its broader importance, and join senior scholars in discussing future technologies.

**Places are limited. Interested doctoral students should email by February 20:ICACHI_public@outlook.com**

**Use the subject line "Doctoral Forum Presentation Application_Name_University" and include your name, institution, supervisor, brief research description, phone number, and WeChat contact.**

The organizing team will contact you as soon as possible after receiving your email.

## Forum Theme

Humanity created technology, but technology also enables and subtly reshapes human thought. As we experience its transformation of modern life, pervasive data and information blur boundaries between reality and illusion and test our spiritual and individual limits. The sudden pandemic has changed social structures and emotional states, revealing humanity's and modern society's vulnerability where technology falls short. We enjoy technological benefits but must also accept their costs. At this unusual moment, when good and bad require redefining and earlier innovations and values need reassessment, we ask early- and mid-career researchers at technology's frontier: beyond better living and greater efficiency, what does technology mean, and where is it going? How can we identify universal values in a rapidly changing world, and what technologies do we need? We also ask young students whether, when established understanding and experience are challenged, they have the ability and courage to propose new interpretations. These questions converge on one concern: what future relationship should we have with technology, and where should humanity's future lead?

## Forum Agenda

## Part 1

Six senior university and industry researchers share broad perspectives on contemporary social and technological issues and future technological directions.

## Part 2

Eight to twelve outstanding doctoral students present frontier research.

## Part 3

Senior researchers and doctoral students discuss Reflections on the Post-AI Era.

The forum aims to open dialogue between mid-career scholars and young people, generating insights through exchanges and seeking needs and meaning for future technological development. It asks whether the culture, thought, and awareness inherited by Chinese descendants can offer Eastern perspectives on technology beyond industrial and information-era paradigms.
