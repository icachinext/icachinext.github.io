## Book Recommendation: Human-Engaged Computing

Xiangshi Ren，Zhiyong Fu，Xiaojuan Ma，Jianmin Wang，Huatong Sun，Xiaojun Yuan，Chen Wang

Authors

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2024-09-29-article-prwbbq8w-01.png)

Centered on the concept of Human-Engaged Computing, this book synthesizes developments in human–computer interaction and offers insights into its future.

The book comprises 11 chapters in two parts: theory and technology. Chapters 1–4 introduce the theoretical framework of Human-Engaged Computing. Reviewing the history of human–computer interaction, they trace the origins of Human-Engaged Computing, examine technology's purposes and developmental trends, and clarify the scope of the Human-Engaged Computing framework. Chapters 5–11 address technology through an HEC perspective, connecting artificial intelligence, digital mindfulness, autonomous driving, information interaction, design futures, and cross-cultural design with Human-Engaged Computing and offering related cases and perspectives.

The book is intended for young students and researchers across disciplines, especially those seeking innovation beyond established frameworks, encouraging reflection on their research positions and future directions.

## Endorsements

### Ning Gu

PhD in Engineering; Professor, School of Computer Science and Technology, Fudan University; Fellow and Honorary Chair of the Cooperative Computing Committee, China Computer Federation

A renowned human–computer interaction scholar, Professor Xiangshi Ren has developed the innovative theoretical framework of Human-Engaged Computing (HEC) through sustained, systematic research.

Within this framework, engaged humans and engaging computers continually develop together and maintain balance through cycles, iterations, and the evolution of synergistic interaction.

This richly detailed book by him and his co-authors spans the history of human–computer interaction and the emergence of HEC, its core elements and framework design, future research directions, theoretical explanations, and numerous practical case studies.

The book deserves close study by researchers and students in human–computer interaction and related fields, and thoughtful reading by everyone concerned with a harmonious future for people, computers, and intelligent technologies.

### Fuji Ren

PhD in Engineering; Chair Professor, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China; Academician of the European Academy of Sciences and the Engineering Academy of Japan; Honorary Vice President, Chinese Association for Artificial Intelligence

Where earlier human–computer interaction discussions addressed the technological requirements of an abstract human-centered approach, Human-Engaged Computing goes further, examining the philosophical premise of what a human being is. It seeks to integrate understandings of people from Eastern and Western cultures, humanities, and social sciences to define the purposes of human–computer interaction technologies.

This book merits study not only by human–computer interaction researchers, but also by students and scholars in other fields developing technologies around people. Particularly in the new era of large-model-driven AI, it promises a new research paradigm for designing interactive technologies with clearer purpose and broader vision.

## Yingqing Xu

PhD in Engineering; Professor, Academy of Arts and Design, and Director, Future Laboratory, Tsinghua University

In recent months, AI technologies exemplified by ChatGPT and GPT have generated successive waves of technological revolution and attracted widespread attention.

We have entered an intelligent society in which human and artificial intelligence are closely integrated—an unprecedented society of coexistence and interdependence between people and machines. Human progress depends on machine intelligence, while machine intelligence needs human collaboration even more. This requires a deeper understanding of what humans are and where they are heading.

At this historical moment, we welcome the publication of Human-Engaged Computing. Written collaboratively by experts and scholars, it comprehensively and systematically introduces the history, core concepts, theories, methods, and applications of Human-Engaged Computing. It is an academic monograph of significant reference value.

### Jun Hu

PhDs in Industrial Design and Interaction Design Engineering; Associate Professor, Eindhoven University of Technology, the Netherlands

I was fortunate to read this book before publication, led by Professor Xiangshi Ren and co-authored by active Chinese scholars in human–computer interaction.

Human-Engaged Computing (HEC) offers a theoretical framework for examining and extending human–computer interaction research. It both builds on and innovates beyond related theories, emphasizing the transcendence of existing human and machine capabilities through synergy. By examining interaction's purpose and nature, the book helps readers organize related theories and deepen their understanding through examples.

With AI advancing rapidly, this book's emphasis on balance and coordination in human–computer interaction makes it especially valuable. Whether seeking a new theoretical framework or inspiration for innovation, readers will find useful guidance and insight.

### Zixue Cheng

## PhD in Engineering; Professor Emeritus and Former Vice President, University of Aizu, Japan

The authors' concept of Human-Engaged Computing is theoretically original and systematic. Their comprehensive work presents an extensive range of concepts, perspectives, and frameworks. Its breadth and depth deserve the attention of human–computer interaction and the computing community as a whole.

Through its exploration of the frontiers of Human-Engaged Computing and its attention to design and technological philosophy, the book centers on human–computer synergy while considering the relationship between humanity and its technological creations. It offers abundant food for thought, stimulating innovative research and helping researchers identify compelling topics.

Immersing myself in this book was exciting. I greatly appreciate the depth of its investigation and the rigor of its discussion. I look forward to its lasting influence on interaction, computing, and design, and to its becoming an important guide for researchers, practitioners, educators, and students.

### Di Liu

## PhD in Law; Professor, Kyorin University, Japan

I warmly congratulate Professor Xiangshi Ren, his colleagues, and students on the forthcoming publication of Human-Engaged Computing. This major work addresses not only the technical issues of Human-Engaged Computing (HEC), but also the relationship between humanity and its creations. This distinctive perspective responds to a widespread contemporary concern and gives the book its transcendent value.

The invention of machines opened a new chapter of human history. In it, people use and control machines while a deep fear of self-alienation continues to grow. Technology kindles human dreams, but also produces anxiety about our limitations and fear of losing control. In today's age of accelerating technological development, we feel an even greater need to reconsider and establish a reasonable mode of coexistence between people and their creations.

The widespread tensions and even antagonism between people and their creations characterize technological and social development today. Professor Ren's team turns to Eastern philosophy to address these contradictions. I hope their exploration will produce recommendations for a reasonable relationship between humanity and its creations and reveal principles applicable to present and future societies.

### Jin Yu

## PhD in Medicine; Professor, Guangzhou University of Chinese Medicine

Interdisciplinary conversations with Professor Ren helped me recognize that synergistic states are everywhere, naturally aligning with traditional Chinese medicine's holistic outlook. Unity of body and spirit and unity of humanity and nature can be understood as synergy between form, spirit, mind, and body, and between human life and its environment, producing harmony and balance.

My proposed State Perspective and Low Dissipative Optimize concepts seek to describe life's self-organizing adjustment and optimized evolution. They share the aim of minimizing antagonism between people and machines, inspiring me to envision creating new ways of life through synergy. People's creativity and synergy with their internal and external living environments are also central to restoring everyday life through rehabilitation, deeply reflecting Eastern cultural values.

As intelligent machines pose major challenges to life, future humanity's deeper awakening and development of consciousness and exploration of mental potential should underpin design thinking. This calls for a worldwide awakening to fundamental principles, rather than seeking the essence of human–machine relationships in techniques of confrontation or comparison shaped by Western thought.

### Peng Cheng

## Founder and CEO, Danish Mental Health Company PauseAble

Rapid advances in computing power and interaction technologies bring efficiency and entertainment, but also capture people's attention and obscure the innate sources of happiness and creativity within human nature.

The book examines people's deep dependence on computers and their simultaneous affection and fear, showing that contemporary human–computer interaction research grounded in Western philosophy has reached a bottleneck that severely constrains human–computer relationships.

The authors creatively introduce Eastern philosophical reflection on lived experience into human–computer interaction research, revealing human potential and fundamental needs more deeply. They also systematically trace the development of human–computer interaction, taking readers on a journey through the pioneers and inventions behind major transformations and recovering human–computer interaction's original purpose of augmenting human thought and wisdom.

Building on this understanding, the book proposes Human-Engaged Computing, opening broad and promising prospects for harmonious coexistence and shared advancement between people and computers.
