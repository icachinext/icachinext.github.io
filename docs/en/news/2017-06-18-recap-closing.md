The fifth International Chinese human–computer interaction Conference took place on June 7–9, 2017, at Guangzhou Baiyun International Convention Center. As the largest professional conference for the Chinese human–computer interaction community, and its first edition in China, it attracted nearly 100 experts and scholars from China and abroad. Almost 300 participants represented 43 universities, companies, and organizations, with interests spanning human–computer interaction, computer graphics, VR/AR, industrial design, media arts, and cognitive psychology.

The conference was organized by International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI) and the School of Art and Design at Guangdong University of Technology, with UXPA China as co-organizer. Sponsors and supporters included China Association for Science and Technology, the Information and Interaction Design Committee of the China Industrial Design Association, Huawei, ACM SIGCHI, the China–Canada International Film Festival, China Computer Federation, Google, Tsinghua University Press, Xinhua's Converged Media Future Research Institute, VisionUnion, Autodesk, and Kingfar. Honorary chairs were Guozhong Dai of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Chengyong Wang of Guangdong University of Technology, Shumin Zhai of Google, Xiangshi Ren of Kochi University of Technology, and Yi-Ping Hung of National Taiwan University. Conference chairs were Yingqing Xu of Tsinghua University and Henry B.L. Duh of La Trobe University. Local organizing chairs were Chengyong Wang, Hai Fang, Xuejiao Huang, Fei Hu, and Kaiping Feng of Guangdong University of Technology. Technical chairs were Yi Ji of Guangdong University of Technology and Xiaojuan Ma of The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology. Program co-chairs were Haipeng Mi of Tsinghua University and Neng-Hao Yu of National Chengchi University.

At around 9 AM, organizers and conference chairs delivered opening remarks. Speakers included Riguang Feng, full-time vice chairman of the Guangdong Association for Science and Technology; Vice President Chengyong Wang of Guangdong University of Technology; and ICACHI President Wei Li. President Wei Li emphasized ICACHI's three goals: strengthening exchanges between Chinese and international human–computer interaction researchers, promoting university–industry collaboration, and supporting practitioners and students. In the keynote session, Xin Tong of Microsoft Research Asia explained the importance of human–computer interaction research in computing. Professor Xiangshi Ren of Japan's Kochi University of Technology, also an honorary conference chair, discussed trends in human–computer interaction and the Human-Engaged Computing (Human-Engaged Computing) vision for its next human–computer interaction wave. Professor Fei Hu, vice dean of the School of Art and Design at Guangdong University of Technology, drew on his experience to discuss the future of interaction design.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2017-06-18-recap-closing-02.png)

Alongside papers and posters, five parallel forums covered innovation and entrepreneurship, intelligent transportation and IoT, interactive art and technology, virtual and augmented reality, and future device human–computer interaction. Fifteen speakers from different fields and regions presented their work, perspectives on current research topics, and interaction design methods, with particular emphasis on future directions for human–computer interaction. To deepen participants' understanding of relevant expertise and emerging topics in human–computer interaction, workshops invited accomplished instructors to teach fundamental human–computer interaction research skills, product and service experience, and design methods.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2017-06-18-recap-closing-03.png)

Experts from Chinese and international universities thoroughly discussed disciplinary development and talent training in China's human–computer interaction community. Overseas experts shared useful experiences and models from their backgrounds and recognized the field's potential in China. As domestic industry increasingly recognizes the value of human–computer interaction and user experience in improving products and services, many companies have created dedicated human–computer interaction positions or even research institutes conducting human–computer interaction research. Industry participants stressed the importance of applying human–computer interaction research to products and their demand for students trained in human–computer interaction. They expressed a strong willingness to improve mutual understanding and communication with academia, providing platforms and support for closer, more specialized integration of industry, education, and research.

human–computer interaction is an interdisciplinary bridge connecting users with technology and services, and one of computing's most important branches. ACM SIGCHI, the field's leading professional group, is ACM's second-largest academic community. ICACHI, the first international professional human–computer interaction organization founded by the Chinese diaspora, was established on May 10, 2012, in Austin during ACM CHI 2012. By building an exchange platform for Chinese scholars worldwide, it strengthens collaboration and academic quality, advances human–computer interaction, and fulfills social responsibilities. The first four Chinese CHI conferences were successfully held in Paris in April 2013, Toronto in April 2014, Seoul in April 2015, and San Jose, California, in May 2016.

ICACHI's members are distributed around the world, and most founders and members are Chinese scholars living in China or overseas. Many of their research contributions have significantly influenced global human–computer interaction. Compared with international developments in human–computer interaction, the discipline remains less established in China. Sharing international perspectives and advanced experience, strengthening collaboration and resource sharing, and supporting Chinese human–computer interaction research and development are therefore responsibilities ICACHI is committed to fulfilling.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2017-06-18-recap-closing-04.png)

The International Chinese human–computer interaction Conference concluded successfully on the evening of June 9, 2017. Masa Inakage, dean of Keio University's Graduate School of Media Design, delivered the closing keynote on creative design. An awards ceremony followed, with honorary chair Xiangshi Ren, conference chairs Yingqing Xu and Henry B.L. Duh, technical chairs Yi Ji and Xiaojuan Ma, and program co-chair Haipeng Mi presenting Best Paper, Best Poster Paper, and video competition awards. Finally, the next conference chair, Professor Zhiyong Fu of Tsinghua University, and Professor Miao Song of Concordia University, president of the China–Canada International Film Festival, announced that the sixth Chinese CHI would take place on April 21–22, 2018, in Montreal, Canada, alongside ACM CHI 2018.

On June 12, shortly after the conference, Premier Li Keqiang emphasized creating a better environment to attract overseas investment and expertise when meeting Chinese diaspora representatives. Participants and organizers affirmed the value of the conference's exchanges and platform, believing Chinese CHI's success would advance human–computer interaction in China. They also expressed confidence that Chinese CHI would become one of the most important platforms for academic and intellectual exchange among Chinese human–computer interaction and computing researchers worldwide. Everyone looks forward to meeting again in Montreal next year!
