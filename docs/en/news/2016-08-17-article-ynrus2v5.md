## IDF16: Intel Unveils Alloy, a Merged Reality Product

Yesterday, August 16, Intel introduced Alloy at IDF16, describing merged reality as VR plus AR plus mixed reality. Its features include:

1. Freedom of movement across six degrees of freedom.
1. Built-in Intel RealSense cameras eliminate the need for external sensors.
1. Direct hand interaction with virtual elements, without controllers.
1. A complete merged reality experience.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2016-08-17-article-ynrus2v5-01.jpg)

Alloy is an all-in-one solution, incorporating computing, sensors, and cameras in one device without a phone or PC connection. Intel also plans to open the hardware and provide APIs for developers and partners in the second half of 2017.

Microsoft calls HoloLens mixed reality; Intel now uses merged reality. Is this another round of new terminology? Whatever the label, interest in VR and AR is evident. From Google Glass to HoloLens and Alloy, all-in-one design seems to be emerging as a trend. Perhaps these devices will one day be as portable and widespread as phones.

## Google Develops Fuchsia: Another Cross-Platform Operating System

Google's newly revealed Fuchsia aims to support connected devices ranging from IoT products and phones to PCs. Its core incorporates recent operating-system technologies and supports ARM and 64-bit Intel x86 architectures.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2016-08-17-article-ynrus2v5-02.jpg)

Google's Internet origins shaped Android for mobile devices and Chrome OS for PCs. Both center on Internet services connecting users. Fuchsia appears intended to bridge mobile devices and PCs, integrating services, applications, and the operating system.

Microsoft has pursued a similar direction. To unify its operating systems, it replaced the longstanding Windows CE mobile architecture with NT-based Windows Mobile and introduced the Universal Windows Platform in 2015, allowing one application to run on PCs and mobile devices. Nevertheless, its phone business has not improved. Google's outcome remains to be seen.

## China Successfully Launches Quantum Satellite Micius

At 01:40 on August 16, 2016, the world's first quantum science experimental satellite, Micius, launched from Jiuquan. It will enable the first satellite-to-ground quantum communication experiments and support an integrated space–ground system for secure communication and scientific research.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2016-08-17-article-ynrus2v5-03.jpg)

Chief scientist and Chinese Academy of Sciences member Pan Jianwei told Xinhua that quantum communication security rests on fundamental physics. The indivisibility of single photons and the impossibility of copying quantum states prevent undetectable interception and decryption, theoretically ensuring unconditional security for authentication, encrypted transmission, and digital signatures and addressing information security at its foundations.

Meanwhile, a quantum laboratory at the University of Science and Technology of China has developed a semiconductor quantum chip, potentially enabling quantum logic operations and information processing. The chip has been demonstrated, but further technical details remain unclear.

## China Introduces Its First Smart Home Appliance Standard

The China Household Electric Appliance Research Institute has released implementation rules and technical specifications for intelligent-index certification of room air conditioners, China's first smart air-conditioning standard. It identifies intelligent functions, characteristics, and utility as three essential dimensions.

First, intelligent functions should address user needs through 24 valuable capabilities across safety, reliability, comfort, ease of use, and energy efficiency.

Second, intelligent characteristics encompass self-learning, adaptation, coordination, diagnosis, inference, organization, and correction to deliver a genuinely intelligent experience.

Third, intelligent utility means using intelligent technologies to add value through safety, reliability, comfort, ease of use, and energy efficiency.
