## Conference Overview

Organized by International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI), the **International Conference on Human-Engaged Computing** (ICHEC 2025) will take place **November 21–23, 2025** at **Singapore Management University** in Singapore. The themes are **Inclusion**, **Intelligence**, and **Connection**.

ICHEC 2025, formerly Chinese CHI, is organized by International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI) and has become an important meeting point connecting HCI researchers, academia, and industry worldwide. Since 2012, Chinese CHI has been held successfully in Paris, Toronto, Seoul, Silicon Valley, Guangzhou, Montreal, Xiamen, Bali, and Shenzhen.

**Past conference papers have been included in the ACM Digital Library and submitted for EI indexing.** Following discussion by the ICACHI Board in 2025, the conference was renamed ICHEC to become more inclusive and attract human–computer interaction researchers worldwide. We invite scholars, researchers, and industry leaders in human–computer interaction and related fields to share findings and experience and explore the possibilities for future human–computer interaction.

### Paper Length

- Writing should be concise, with length proportional to the contribution. Word counts include only the main text, excluding references, appendices, and figure and table captions.
- **Full papers:** Between 5,000 and 12,000 words, with **7,000–8,000 words recommended**.
- **Short papers:** **No more than 5,000 words**, single-column, excluding references.
- **Full papers over 12,000 words and short papers over 5,000 words will be desk-rejected** unless authors provide compelling justification for the additional length.
- **Use single-column formatting for submission** and the official conference template. Other templates may result in desk rejection. LaTeX and Overleaf templates default to two columns; use \documentclass[manuscript, screen, review]{acmart} to switch to single-column format.

### Use of Generative AI

- ACM permits generative AI tools in preparing paper content. **AI-assisted writing or polishing, or use beyond editing the authors' own text, must be fully disclosed in the acknowledgments.** LaTeX and Overleaf templates hide acknowledgments by default; ensure they are visible in the submission if disclosure is required. Generative AI software must not be listed as an author. Authors remain fully responsible for all content, including AI-generated material.
- Since 2024, ACM has required **AI-content screening for all papers intended for publication.** ACM designates **Morressier as its official screening provider** (www.morressier.com) to ensure reliable results. Authors may screen manuscripts before submission or after acceptance and revise them according to recommendations until publication standards are met.

### Use of Generative AI Tools

Following the policy of ACM, **the use of generative AI tools to create content is permitted, but it must be fully disclosed in the acknowledgements section of the paper when such tools are used for purposes beyond editing the author’s own text.**

Note that the LaTeX template defaults to hiding the acknowledgements section while in review mode – please make sure that any LLM disclosure is available in your submitted draft. Generative AI software tools should not be listed as authors even if they are used in the preparation of a paper. Authors will take all responsibility for all content in their paper, including any content that may have been generated with AI.
