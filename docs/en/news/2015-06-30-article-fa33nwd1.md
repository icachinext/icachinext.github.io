## Xie Guozhong / XieGuozhong

"The human–computer interaction conference is a dynamic, promising, and cohesive community. It provides a high-quality, open platform for researchers around the world who are interested in human–computer interaction. Many researchers from this community have already achieved outstanding results that will profoundly influence people's everyday lives, now and in the future."

Guozhong Dai is a distinguished professor and doctoral supervisor at Institute of Software, Chinese Academy of Sciences, where he established the human–computer interaction Laboratory in 1999. Professor Guozhong Dai graduated from the University of Science and Technology of China and was a visiting scholar in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Maryland from 1982 to 1985. His principal interests are human–computer interaction design, computer graphics, and virtual reality, including pen-based interaction, natural user interfaces, and 3D interfaces. A pioneer in human–computer interaction, Professor Guozhong Dai led early research on pen-based interaction in China. In 2014, he received a contribution award from the Chinese computer graphics conference and a lifetime achievement award from the 10th Joint Conference on Harmonious Human–Machine Environments.

Professor Guozhong Dai chairs the Chinese computer association's human–computer interaction committee and is deputy chair of the National Technical Committee on Ergonomics Standardization (SAC/TC159). He is also deputy chair of the architecture, communications, and integration frameworks subcommittee of the national industrial automation systems and integration standardization committee, and deputy chair of the China Simulation Federation's digital entertainment committee. Professor Guozhong Dai has led or participated in projects funded by the National Natural Science Foundation of China, the National High-Tech R&D Program, and the National Basic Research Program. He has authored or co-authored three academic books and published more than 200 journal and conference papers. His honors include three National Science and Technology Progress Awards, multiple Chinese Academy of Sciences and ministerial science and technology awards, and recognition in 1993 as an outstanding young or middle-aged scientist of the Chinese Academy of Sciences.

Keywords: pen-based interfaces; multimodal interfaces; interaction paradigms

## Shihai Dong / DongShihai

"China's human–computer interaction community is developing rapidly. Let us seize this opportunity together, catch up with and surpass international advances, and keep moving forward! I look forward to the ingenuity and interdisciplinary innovation of the younger generation."

Shihai Dong, born in September 1939, is a retired professor and doctoral supervisor in the School of Computer Science at Peking University. He visited the University of Maryland's Department of Computer Science in 1982 and became director of Peking University's Graphics and human–computer interaction Technology Laboratory in 1984. During 1995-1998, he led a key National Natural Science Foundation project on multimodal user interfaces. He also served as deputy chair of China Computer Federation's virtual reality and visualization technology committee. His books include Computer User Interfaces and Their Tools (1994) and human–computer interaction and Multimodal User Interfaces (1999). He reviewed for CAD&Graphics and was deputy editor of the software section of the Encyclopedia of Computer Science and Technology. In 2014, he received a lifetime achievement award from the 10th Joint Conference on Harmonious Human–Machine Environments in China.

Keywords: multimodal user interfaces; virtual reality; visualization; software tools

## Yuanchun Shi / ShiYuanchun

"human–computer interaction studies how to optimize computing around the user."

Yuanchun Shi is a professor in the Department of Computer Science at Tsinghua University, director of the Institute of human–computer interaction and Media Integration, and head of ubiquitous computing research at the Tsinghua National Laboratory for Information Science and Technology. Her research focuses on human–computer interaction, ubiquitous computing, and multimedia communications. She has developed intelligent spaces, multimodal interaction systems, ubiquitous computing systems, and networked multimedia engineering, supporting interface effectiveness and optimization models, very large multiuser interfaces, and multimedia multicast protocols. She has published in IJHCS, IEEE ubiquitous computing venues, IEEE TPDS, IEEE TKDE, and ACM conferences including human–computer interaction (CHI), MobileHCI, MM, and UIST. Her papers received CHI honorable mentions in 2013 and 2015 and a MobileHCI best paper award in 2013, including the first such awards for mainland Chinese universities at CHI, UIST, and IEEE ubiquitous computing conferences. She teaches three undergraduate and graduate courses on human–computer interaction. Professor Yuanchun Shi earned her bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees in computer science at Tsinghua University and joined Tsinghua University's computer science faculty in 1993. Supporting education in western China, she has also served as head of Qinghai University's computer science department. Her editorial roles include regional editor of Pervasive and Mobile Computing (Elsevier, 2010-2014), editor of Interacting with Computer (IwC, Oxford University Press, 2011–), and deputy editor of Communications of CCF (China Computer Federation, 2012–). She also co-chaired UbiComp 2011, the 13th ACM International Conference on Ubiquitous Computing, and other international conferences.

Keywords: intelligent spaces; computer desktops; interaction efficiency models; activity recognition

## Yongtian Wang / WangYongtian

"human–computer interaction technologies can help people understand and manage digital information better, an increasingly important task in the age of big data."

Yongtian Wang earned a bachelor's degree in precision instrumentation from Tianjin University in 1982 and a PhD in optics from the University of Reading in 1986. He is a professor of optics and applied computing at Beijing Institute of Technology, a Ministry of Education Changjiang Distinguished Professor, director of Beijing Institute of Technology's information and electronics division, and director of the Beijing Engineering Research Center for Mixed Reality and Advanced Displays. He also heads the Ministry of Science and Technology's virtual reality and multimedia expert group; is a senior member of SPIE, OSA, and IET; serves on the Chinese Optical Society council; and is deputy head of the human–computer interaction group of China Computer Federation. His research covers optical imaging and illumination design and CAD, interactive 3D displays, image processing, and virtual and augmented reality applications. He has published more than 400 papers and holds more than 70 patents. His team's augmented reality and interactive display systems have been applied in culture, education, entertainment, medicine, and industry across more than 20 Chinese provinces.

Keywords: augmented reality; see-through displays; head-mounted displays; interactive displays; true 3D displays

## Qianying Wang / WangQianying

"It is wonderful that CHI is coming to Asia! It will inspire so much innovation!"

Qianying Wang heads Lenovo's Emerging Devices and User Research division. Her team's deep understanding of emerging devices, additional services, and solutions has helped Lenovo establish leadership in the Internet of Things, intelligent devices, and computing platforms. As executive director of the emerging devices division, she leads the development of Lenovo's user-centered approach. She and her user experience team support the company's mobile Internet device strategy, including smartphones, tablets, smart TVs, and tablet computing.

Before joining Lenovo, Qianying Wang was a researcher at IBM Research China, where her contributions to research and product development earned two IBM awards. She holds a PhD from Stanford University. Her interests include collaborative environments from sociological and psychological perspectives, novel handheld input devices, and ubiquitous computing. She holds more than 30 patents and has published more than 20 international journal papers.

Keywords: human–computer interaction; user experience innovation; Internet of Things; ubiquitous computing

## Xiangshi Ren / RenXiangshi

"Over the past three decades, human–computer interaction technologies have profoundly affected research and industry. Yet current human–computer interaction paradigms still provide only partial coordination rather than close integration between people and computers. Our world also faces many problems, and some experts even question humanity's survival. What can human–computer interaction contribute? I believe we need highly integrated, efficient human–computer interaction to help solve complex global problems. We therefore propose a new perspective on human–computer interaction: Human-Engaged Computing. human–computer interaction emphasizes a more comprehensive understanding of people to achieve closer synergy between human and technological capabilities and greater effectiveness. We believe human engagement is essential to this synergy, but its potential remains largely untapped."

Xiangshi Ren directs the human–computer interaction Institute in the School of Information at Kochi University of Technology and is president of International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI). He has been a visiting researcher at IBM Almaden and a chair professor at the University of Toronto and several Chinese universities. He is a senior member of ACM and IEEE. With more than 20 years of fundamental research in human–computer interaction, he is recognized as a pioneer in the field. He has received more than 30 research grants, serving as principal investigator on 25, and 19 international awards. He has delivered invited talks at numerous international conferences and served as a reviewer, associate editor, guest editor, and program committee member. Ten students have completed doctorates under his supervision. His research, academic service, professional capabilities, and international connections make him a bridge between Eastern and Western scientific communities.

Keywords: human–computer interaction; user interface design; interaction design; mobile algorithms; accessibility; human performance models; pen, gesture, eye-tracking, gaming, and haptic interaction research; human–computer interaction computing

## Shumin Zhai / ZhaiShumin

"Science, innovation, and the collision of ideas—how fortunate we are to work in a field where we can put them into practice."

Shumin Zhai is a senior researcher at Google Research, specializing in fundamental and applied human–computer interaction, user interface design, and related technological developments. From 1996 to 2010, he worked at IBM Almaden on product innovation and fundamental interface research. He pioneered the SHARK/ShapeWriter gesture keyboard, establishing a new approach to touchscreen text entry, obtaining its first patent, and publishing its first paper with his doctoral student Per Ola Kristensson. In 2004, he released the first gesture keyboard through IBM alphaWorks, followed by ShapeWriter WritingPad, which ranked sixth among iPhone applications. At Google, he and his colleagues continued advancing smartphone keyboards. His papers have received an ACM UIST lasting impact award and an IEEE Computer Society best paper award. He has served on many academic committees and is editor-in-chief of leading journals, including ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction. He has visited or held guest professorships at universities in the United States, Europe, and China. In 1977, after the Cultural Revolution, he entered university through the first reinstated national entrance examination. He began his academic career after completing graduate studies in 1984, went abroad in 1989, and received his PhD from the University of Toronto in 1995. ACM named him a Distinguished Scientist in 2006, and he became an ACM and CHI fellow in 2010. He is also an adviser and honorary president of International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction.

Keywords: interaction modalities; input methods; human performance models; user interfaces

## Henry B.L. Duh / DuBenlin

"human–computer interaction research has a long history in the Asia-Pacific region. Its work is distinctive and diverse, spanning computing, engineering, social science, and design, with extensive contributions to journals and conference proceedings. Despite cultural, linguistic, disciplinary, and geographical differences, CHI's first visit to Asia in 2015 offers local researchers the chance to meet and learn from HCI experts worldwide, while opening opportunities for future regional collaboration. I hope participants will benefit from CHI 2015's rich program and find sparks of new inspiration."

Professor Duh directs Australia's information and communications technology institute and the Human Interface Technology Laboratory, a joint center involving the University of Washington, the University of Tasmania, and the Tasmanian Government. He is also a professor in the University of Tasmania's School of Engineering and ICT. Originally from Taiwan, he holds degrees in psychology, industrial design, and engineering and completed postdoctoral research at NASA's Johnson Space Center. He previously taught at Nanyang Technological University's interactive and digital media institute and at National University of Singapore.

Professor Duh works closely with human–computer interaction communities and business partners, influencing technology industry policy across the Asia-Pacific. He is a member of IET and BCS, a senior member of ACM and IEEE, and represents Australia on IFIP TC13 for human–computer interaction and TC14 for entertainment computing. He chairs the steering committee of International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction, serves on the editorial board of Springer's human–computer interaction book series, and edits its series on media industries and social impact. He is also a member of a Singapore government think tank and has published more than 100 journal and conference papers in human–computer interaction.

Keywords: augmented and virtual reality; interaction design and media; entertainment computing and the effects of games
