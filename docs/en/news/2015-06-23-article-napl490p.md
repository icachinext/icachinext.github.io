## 1 Introducing ICACHI

International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI) was founded on May 10, 2012, in Austin, USA, during ACM CHI 2012. Its board comprises 27 overseas Chinese representatives, including two supervisors, who work actively around the world in Human Computer Interaction (human–computer interaction).

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2015-06-23-article-napl490p-02.jpg)

human–computer interaction is like air and water: it is present in every corner of our lives and work. This interdisciplinary field studies the design, evaluation, and implementation of interactive computing systems for human use, together with the major phenomena surrounding them. The field and its applications are essential to computer science, information science, and many other disciplines. Its purpose is to enable faster, better communication between humans and computers, or between people through computers. human–computer interaction is therefore indispensable to the healthy development of the information society in the 21st century. More broadly, human–computer interaction is the science of how people interact with the entire information space. By connecting computer-generated virtual worlds with reality, the field will continue to expand and deepen, producing more applications that change the world. We believe that, through the efforts of the board and all members, both the association and the field have an even brighter future.

## 3 Future Priorities

The association should focus on work that can only be accomplished collectively. Almost every board member already has a specific project. These and other future priorities are listed below for your support and discussion.

- Policy Recommendations

Our association can offer advice and policy recommendations to governments in members' countries and regions. My contribution to this issue is a first step. I hope future newsletters will feature members' voices and articles that resonate with our community. The association can revise these articles and, following board approval, submit them to the relevant authorities. When the time is right, we could establish a policy advisory committee. Drawing on experience from different countries and regions—one of our membership's strengths—we can develop recommendations suited to local circumstances. For example, we could jointly advocate using overseas expertise to establish a national HCI strategic research institute or joint laboratories connecting overseas talent.

- Joint Publications

Our members' research covers nearly every aspect of human–computer interaction. The association can use wikis and other online tools to organize and edit printed or electronic books on human–computer interaction. Board member Shengdong Zhao has already begun work in this area. A publications committee could be established as needed to develop a publication program.

- Joint Lecture Series

We can also draw on members' diverse resources to organize lecture series. Xiaojuan Ma and other board members are already working on this.

- Academic Committee

As the association and its membership grow, we can establish an academic committee and consider forming relevant special interest groups.

- External Engagement

We should actively participate in academic exchanges in our countries and regions. As Asian economies continue to develop, peer review and project assessment are becoming increasingly international. In China, for example, the government has decided to step back from directly managing science and technology projects, creating a need for professional organizations to participate. Our association can play a larger and more active role. Our familiarity with developments around the world can help reviewers avoid funding projects that merely repeat work already done abroad, preventing substantial waste. We can also recommend overseas talent to governments, introduce technology projects to governments and businesses, and cooperate with universities and research institutes.

- ICACHI Awards

We can invite people from all sectors who strongly support the association to serve as advisers and establish awards. Chinese CHI already offers Best Paper and Best Student Paper awards, providing an excellent starting point. With fair selection procedures and reference to awards from related associations, we could introduce ICACHI awards for service and contributions, honorary directors, and honorary membership. We could also recognize, for example, ten leading Chinese scholars worldwide in human–computer interaction. Wei Li and board member Liu Li have already begun work on an ICACHI student competition.
