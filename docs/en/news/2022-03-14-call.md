## About the Program

The second HCI·X Summer Research Program in 2022 will again be jointly organized by leading human–computer interaction professors at City University of Hong Kong, the University of Wisconsin–Madison, University of Maryland Baltimore County, University of Notre Dame, University of Waterloo, Duke Kunshan University, Northwestern Polytechnical University, and other institutions in China and abroad.

The program helps outstanding students understand frontier human–computer interaction topics, gain practical research experience, learn human–computer interaction methods and approaches, and explore emerging human–computer interaction technologies to address important social, technological, and humanistic problems. Topics span human–computer interaction, including emerging interactive systems, interaction design, voice interaction, social computing, AR/VR, accessibility, privacy and security, visualization, and interactive AI.

Through the Program, Students Can:

1) Learn human–computer interaction design and research methodology, understand and practice quantitative, qualitative, and other human–computer interaction research and design methods, and learn academic writing in human–computer interaction;

2) Receive faculty recommendation letters based on strong performance;

3) Discuss PhD applications in depth with mentors, potentially receiving preliminary offers;

4) Prepare papers for leading journals and conferences under faculty guidance, with authorship ordered by contribution;

5) Join seminars, workshops, and graduate/PhD application Q&A sessions organized by mentors from leading universities and collaborate with other aspiring human–computer interaction researchers;

6) Develop networks for international graduate applications and research collaboration.

The HCI·X Summer Program 2022 will recruit remote interns for mentors' human–computer interaction projects. Applicants must commit full-time for at least three months, with a minimum of 30 hours weekly. The first application deadline is March 31 and the second April 30. Mentors will interview applicants in April–May, and the faculty team will determine results collectively.

## HCI·X Faculty

Mentors graduated from Cornell, Carnegie Mellon, Stanford, Toronto, UCL, UCLA, Waterloo, and other leading universities and now hold assistant or associate professorships at the organizing institutions. ## Can Liu

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2022-03-14-call-01.png)

Dr. Can Liu (https://sweb.cityu.edu.hk/canliu/index.html) is an assistant professor at the School of Creative Media, City University of Hong Kong. Before joining CityU, she was a postdoc researcher at the University College London, and obtained her PhD in HCI from INRIA Saclay and the University of Paris-11. Her research is about developing future interfaces based on studies of human natural behavior in areas including voice interfaces, cross-display and spatial interfaces. Can has 10 years of track record of publishing at top HCI conferences and journals, is a winner of Google Faculty Research Award, SIGCHI Best Paper and Honourable Mention award. She has been a program committee member and reviewer for CHI conferences and serves in the organizational committees of CHI, UIST, ISS and Chinese CHI.

**Research areas:** Natural User Interface, Voice/Multimodal interface, Cross-device Interaction, 3D Manipulation, Remote Work/Life in Metaverse

## Zhicong Lu

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2022-03-14-call-02.png)

## Xin Tong

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2022-03-14-call-04.png)

Dr. Xin Tong (https://xintong.ca/) is an Assistant Professor of Computation and Design at Duke Kunshan University. Previously, Xin worked as a postdoc fellow at Stanford University with funding from the Canadian NSERC postdoc fellowship. She received her Ph.D. in School of Interactive Arts and Technology, Simon Fraser University, Canada. Her work largely centers on understanding, designing, developing, and evaluating interactive and intelligent technologies in the field of Human-Computer Interaction (HCI), such as VR/AR, games, social computing, and AI. She deeply embeds a design research approach, working in partnership with parents/children, older adults, clinicians, caregivers, and patients frequently to solve their problems and reach their goals through a user-centered framework. Xin’s work has been published at top-tier conferences and journals, including CHI, CSCW, JMIR, Frontiers, PervasiveHealth, and so on. Further, she has been committee chairs for CSCW and CHI and an expert reviewer for CHI, CSCW, DIS, JMIR, Frontiers, Nature’s Scientific Reports, etc. Recently, Xin has been working on AI-generated virtual agents, pets, and environments for personalized learning and wellbeing (e.g., autistic children, older adults, and general stress-management), plant(nature)-human interaction, VR rehab, and AR co-creation art project.

**Research areas:** XR, Games and Play, Virtual Agents (Human and Pet), Wellbeing, Aging and Health, Social Computing, NFT and Blockchain, Design, Human-Nature Interaction, Tangible Computing

## Program Content

The program researches frontier human–computer interaction topics, matching mentors, research directions, and interns through mutual selection. On the application form, list one to three preferred mentors and up to three research areas of interest.

## Expected Outcomes

Under faculty guidance, research results will be developed into papers and submitted for review and publication at leading international human–computer interaction or related venues, including ACM CHI, CSCW, UIST, UbiComp, WWW, DIS, IUI, CHI PLAY, TEI, ISS, and SOUPS.
