ICACHI Editor's Note: Two weeks have passed since CHI 2016, a major event in human–computer interaction. A postdoctoral researcher in the editor's laboratory who attended has shared a recap. Although individual research interests shape such accounts, even a partial view offers insight. We hope readers enjoy it and welcome contributions sharing your own CHI experiences.

## By the Numbers

- 3,800 attendees
- 50% attending for the first time
- More than 200 papers
- More than 40 sessions
- More than 1,200 lunch@chi participants
- More than 700 volunteer hours

## Trends

- The human–computer interaction community is growing rapidly and expanding its scope, increasingly integrating areas such as networking, signal processing, politics, economics, privacy, and security.
- Current human–computer interaction topics include input and interaction, a longstanding interest now extending to smartwatches; fabrication; social computing and behavior; health; behavior change; user experience; and modeling.
- Combining human–computer interaction with machine learning may become an important trend. Human-centered machine learning brings HCI and ML methods together to address big-data security, visualization, behavior mining, and customized interfaces.

## Recommended Papers

One-Dimensional Handwriting: Inputting Letters and Words on Smart Glasses

This paper maps one-dimensional gestures on Google Glass to two-dimensional strokes for text entry. From Tsinghua University, it offers an interesting and imaginative approach.

DualKey: Miniature Screen Text Entry via Finger Identification

Wearables are increasingly popular, particularly smartwatches. This paper implements text entry by placing two letters on one virtual key: tapping with the index finger selects the left letter, and the middle finger selects the right. Sensors distinguish the fingers.

IJQwerty: What Difference Does One Key Change Make? Gesture Typing Keyboard Optimization Bounded by One Key Position Change from Qwerty

What happens when two letters on a keyboard switch positions, or when a conventional keyboard is unsuitable? Read this paper by Xiaojun Bi and Shumin Zhai.

Pre-Touch Sensing for Mobile Interaction

An impressive Microsoft technology: a self-capacitance touchscreen enables interaction while the finger hovers above it. Microsoft's phones may not sell well, but its technological inventions remain remarkable.

Local Standards for Sample Size at CHI

A paper recommended for everyone in human–computer interaction, analyzing experimental sample sizes in CHI 2014 papers and offering a fresh perspective on study design.
