## ICACHI Online Forum

As society and the economy slowly restart after the global pandemic's pause, we seek to confront humanity's future challenges and reconsider technology's role in society. We aim to envision more forward-looking, resilient, and inclusive human–computer interaction models, develop applications and solutions for known and unknown problems, inspire reflection on progress, and strengthen crisis response.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-09-forum-02.png)

**Session 3: Interaction Technologies for the Future Internet of Things**

The arrival of 5G marks a new era in information technology. Developments in mobile and IoT interaction technologies have driven explosive growth in global computing and transformed everyday life. Over the past decade, increasing numbers of connected devices—including smart speakers, smart TVs, and home sensors—have entered our homes and significantly improved quality of life.

Yet interaction with smart home devices still needs improvement. As more connected devices enter our living spaces, using them becomes substantially more complex. Many barriers remain in cross-device experiences, and achieving continuous, natural interaction across devices and contexts requires overcoming numerous human–computer interaction challenges.

This forum discusses new sensing and interaction technologies. Through research and applications involving multimodality, AI, AR, and VR, smart devices may soon perceive both their own and their users' spatial environments. Advances in human–computer interaction for IoT will continue driving technological transformation and IT industry development.

## Presentation by Dr. Wei Li

**Topic:** Future UI for the Internet of Things

**Abstract:** We are accustomed to controlling individual computing devices with mice, keyboards, and touchscreens. Growing numbers of IoT products create new human–computer interaction challenges across multiple devices: complexity as systems expand; connectivity between device types; consistent interfaces across different form factors; continuous, natural cross-device task experiences; and awareness of users' environments. This talk examines current IoT interaction technologies and future human–computer interaction trends, introducing our recent work on multimodal contactless interaction.

21:00

## Presentation by Huawei Tu

**Topic:** human–computer interaction Design Based on the Crossing Paradigm

**Abstract:** Crossing has become an important paradigm in human–computer interaction design, selecting targets by moving across them. Its efficient selection, rich expressiveness, and compatibility with clicking and gestures have supported extensive research in mouse, pen, and touch input. As VR, AR, and IoT diversify human–computer interaction, crossing's potential remains to be explored. This talk examines three ways to strengthen its expressiveness in human–computer interaction design: fundamental research and applications in VR, theoretical work on moving-target selection, and crossing-based interaction with multiple targets. It concludes with future directions.

21:20

## Presentation by Da-Yuan Huang

**Topic:** Enhancing Interfaces Through Tactile Perception and Feedback

**Abstract:** As mobile devices and IoT develop, people interact with computing devices of varied sizes and forms across daily contexts. Providing convenient, immersive experiences is a key interface design challenge. I will discuss combining an understanding of human tactile perception with multimodal sensing to improve input and device control, using small devices such as watches and smart gloves as examples. Moving from input to output, I will examine haptic feedback for multimedia interaction: how to design easily perceived, unobtrusive feedback that enhances immersion. I will share hardware prototypes, design considerations, and applications of different feedback modules to IoT furniture, VR equipment, and styluses.

21:40

## Presentation by Xing-Dong Yang

**Topic:** Building Interaction Systems for Smart Objects

**Abstract:** Every computing device needs an interaction system, such as a touchscreen. But when everyday objects—clothing, accessories, furniture, and even our bodies—gain computing and intelligence, conventional rectangular touchscreens may no longer fit. Smart objects may be flat, curved, or flexible. Technological innovation is bringing them into our lives, with tremendous opportunities and challenges. I will discuss three challenges in building their interfaces: sensing hardware, software interface design, and energy consumption. I will also present preliminary work addressing each: a textile sensor for contextual interaction that lets tablecloths and sofas recognize commonly contacting objects such as keys and phones; a QWERTY keyboard on the index fingertip for touch-typing English with the thumb; and a self-powered touchpad for small objects, using solar cells for both power and gesture sensing. These projects demonstrate initial approaches to all three challenges. If time permits, I will share other intriguing projects from our lab.
