## Biography

Dr. Michelle Zhou is co-founder and CEO of Juji, a US AI startup that enables people to build and operate cognitive AI assistants without coding. Before founding Juji, she spent 15 years at IBM Research and IBM Watson, leading research and development of human-centered AI technologies and solutions. An ACM Distinguished Scientist, she received her undergraduate degree in computer science from Fudan University and her PhD in computer science from Columbia University.

Dr. Zhou researches human-centered AI, an interdisciplinary field combining artificial intelligence and human–computer interaction. Her focus is intelligent user interaction (IUI), including empowering conversational AI agents with human soft skills and automatically generating interactive multimodal data stories. She has published over 100 papers, applied for over 45 patents, and developed more than a dozen widely used products and services, including IBM Watson Personality Insights. She now studies no-code AI, allowing people to create cognitive AI assistants without programming. She also advances AI applications in education, healthcare, and talent management, working to bring intelligent user interaction into the mainstream.

From ACM student member to Distinguished Member, Dr. Zhou has helped build the global intelligent user interaction community, connecting academia, industry, and government. An early contributor to intelligent human–AI interaction research in China, she actively supports researchers at Chinese institutions. She was the first member-elected steering committee chair of ACM's International Conference on Intelligent User Interfaces, has organized or chaired over 60 leading ACM AI and human–computer interaction conferences and workshops, and has served on the editorial boards of three ACM journals. She is currently editor-in-chief of ACM Transactions on Interactive Intelligent Systems (TiiS), promoting awareness and development of human-centered AI, including explainability, ethics, and safety.

## Election Manifesto

Throughout my career, I have regarded ACM as another home outside my workplace. ACM has helped my career and connected me with professionals around the world, many of whom have become friends, collaborators, mentors, and students. I am honored to be nominated for the ACM Council and hope to serve the broader community by expanding ACM's influence and leadership in two ways:

- Welcome people from more fields into the ACM family. I want ACM to be a home for more people, including entrepreneurs and innovators alongside computing professionals. As editor-in-chief of ACM TiiS, for example, I created a column featuring leaders at the intersection of AI and human–computer interaction, offering entrepreneurs perspectives on human-centered AI. I also introduced Practitioners' Toolbox, a submission category for people outside AI and IT. My multidisciplinary background and extensive collaboration with academia, industry, and government can help broaden ACM's opportunities and reach.
- Build an ACM with greater leadership and empathy. While ACM already leads in shaping the IT industry and STEM education, it can also shape society in many other areas: establishing standards to assess AI performance and safety, developing policies for strong and healthy computing technology, and defining new curricula through which non-STEM students can learn computing skills. My entrepreneurial experience and collaboration with supporters such as K–12 educators can help ACM lead in these areas.
