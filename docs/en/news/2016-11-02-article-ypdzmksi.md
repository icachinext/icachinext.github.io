## A Clear Stream and Distant Southern Mountains

Professor Xiangshi Ren's warmth and smile eased my nerves at our first meeting. As I introduced myself, he took out a notebook and carefully recorded key details. His seriousness moved and humbled me. I later learned that everyone in the laboratory kept such a notebook for research information. He exemplified learning and conduct alike.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2016-11-02-article-ypdzmksi-01.png)

Last October, I came to Kochi University of Technology for an exchange and experienced life in a Japanese town. Kami, where the university is located, is quiet and unhurried. People at the university are similarly composed, yet purposeful and orderly, their eyes reflecting work and ideals. Professor Ren later explained his aspiration: for his research to make the world better, however slightly.

He often admired Kochi's beauty: layered mountains and drifting clouds resembling an ink painting. I nodded, thinking that this was how mountains should be.

## Peach and Plum Trees Do Not Speak, Yet a Path Forms Beneath Them

First, an introduction: the Center for Human-Engaged Computing (CHEC) at Kochi University of Technology is a renowned, forward-looking institute in human–computer interaction, led by Professor Xiangshi Ren, lifetime honorary president of ICACHI.

Professor Xiangshi Ren has conducted basic and applied human–computer interaction research since 1991. He founded the human–computer interaction Technology Laboratory (Ren Lab) at Kochi University of Technology in 2000 and the human–computer interaction institute, the Center for Human-Computer Interaction, in 2012. In 2013, he proposed Human-Engaged Computing (HEC), emphasizing technology's role in developing human wisdom and capabilities. The theory appeared in the August 2016 issue of Computer, the flagship magazine of IEEE, one of the world's largest professional technical organizations.

[http://xrenlab.com/sites/default/files/r8mag-HEC-IEEE%20final-July-20.pdf](http://xrenlab.com/sites/default/files/r8mag-HEC-IEEE%20final-July-20.pdf)

The institute is one of Kochi University of Technology's seven centers, the only one with an English name and the only one directed by a non-Japanese professor.

CHEC laboratory group photograph

CHEC brings together members from Japan, China, Iran, Egypt, Myanmar, India, Thailand, and Finland. Research discussion fills the laboratory, from corners to whiteboards. Former members tend to be proactive in work and study. Many have pursued positions or further study at leading institutions, including the University of Tokyo, Swansea University, Renmin University of China, Jilin University, and Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics.

CHEC recently hosted IxAP/IDHF 2016, bringing together more than 120 researchers from as many as 18 countries to discuss advances in human–computer interaction technologies for older adults.

## Looking Up to High Mountains and Following the Broad Road

On arriving at CHEC, I felt humbled by everyone's talent, diligence, and pursuit of excellence. I remember my nervous first philosophical discussion in English, my rushed first laboratory presentation, and discovering others' rigorous reasoning and dedication. Even if their standard seemed distant, it gave me a goal to strive toward.

The laboratory requires drafts a month before submission, leaving time for members to review one another's work and seek advice from doctoral and postdoctoral researchers. Before this CHI deadline, Professor Ren asked us to complete one section per week. With no prior paper-writing experience, especially in English, my best efforts still contained many problems. Colleagues patiently helped revise them. Professor Ren then reviewed our drafts word by word, down to punctuation.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2016-11-02-article-ypdzmksi-04.png)

Group photograph at a strawberry farm in Kochi

Life beyond research was varied: spring picnics under cherry blossoms and discussions of life; summer barbecues, undeterred by smoke-blackened faces; autumn vineyard visits, reflecting on the year's gains and losses while eating grapes; and winter celebrations combining year-end gatherings with New Year parties.

My most memorable outing was a group walk following a river from the mountains to its mouth. At the coast, river water persistently met waves washing onto the shore. On the return, the slow-setting sun moved me. Its change was imperceptible moment by moment, yet once it disappeared behind the mountains, the earth fell quiet. People and the world alike change gradually. How precious and powerful those small changes are. I began to understand my teacher's aspiration.

I have been back at my own university for nearly a month. Remembering the past year brings an involuntary smile and a little sadness.

Finally, I thank Professor Ren for this exchange opportunity and lessons in both work and character that will benefit me throughout life. I also sincerely thank Chaky, Jason Zhenxin, and Kavous. Although this is no awards ceremony and they may never read it, their generosity and CHEC's support made a foreign country feel warm. I also recommend Kochi University of Technology's Special Scholarship Program (SSP).

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2016-11-02-article-ypdzmksi-05.png)

This three-year doctoral program provides a monthly scholarship of JPY 150,000, approximately RMB 10,000. Doctoral students at Kochi University of Technology can therefore focus on research rather than tuition and living expenses. Instruction is in English, with optional Japanese classes also available.
