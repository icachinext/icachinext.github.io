## Interaction Design Workshops

The **Chinese CHI 2023 conference**, organized by International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI), will take place **November 13–16, 2023**, in **Bali, Indonesia**, in a hybrid format. The theme is **Generative, Reflect, and Envision**.

Everyone is welcome to join these workshops. If you have registered for the conference, scan the QR code on the poster to sign up. Otherwise, register for the conference first, then scan the code.

## Workshop Program

## Traditional Culture Workshop: Where New Contexts Meet Ancient Grammar

Workshop 1

- **Capacity:** 8–15 participants
- **Time:** November 13, 2023, 08:00AM–12:00AM
- **Venue:** Southeast Asia Center, Tsinghua University
- **Format:** In person
- **Description:** The workshop examines the effectiveness and challenges of human–computer interaction in innovations involving traditional Chinese culture. It explores human–computer interaction methods, materials, and ideas, their influence on traditional Chinese culture, and the challenges they may introduce.
- **Organizers:** Associate Professor Haipeng Mi, Tsinghua University, and Associate Professor Yingying She, Xiamen University

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2023-11-10-chinesechi2023-workshop-registration-02.jpg)

**Enhancing Accessible Design Tools: Value Integration in the Design Process**

Workshop 2

- **Capacity:** 10–15 participants
- **Time:** November 13, 2023, 08:00AM–12:00AM
- **Venue:** Southeast Asia Center, Tsinghua University
- **Format:** In person
- **Description:** This workshop provides a platform for understanding and discussing the importance of accessible design and ways to achieve it. Practical experience upgrading design tools will ultimately promote innovation in design.
- **Organizers:** Associate Professor Yun Wang, Beihang University, and Dr. Weiwei Zhang, Tsinghua University

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2023-11-10-chinesechi2023-workshop-registration-03.jpg)

## Co-Designing Human–Chatbot Interaction in Healthcare

Workshop 3

- **Capacity:** 8–15 participants
- **Time:** November 13, 2023, 02:00PM–05:15PM
- **Venue:** Southeast Asia Center, Tsinghua University
- **Format:** In person
- **Description:** Introduces how healthcare chatbots' social characteristics and communication modalities affect users' privacy concerns, trust, and technology acceptance, and explains human–chatbot interaction co-design methods. Participants use co-design tools to discuss and develop healthcare chatbot design frameworks and guidelines and apply them in practice.
- **Organizers:** Qingchuan Li, Xin Chen, and Jiaxin Zhang, Harbin Institute of Technology (Shenzhen), Shenzhen University, and Southern University of Science and Technology

## Panel Program

## Interdisciplinary Integration in human–computer interaction and Models of Industry–Academia–Research Collaboration

- **Time:** November 13, 2023, 01:00PM–03:10PM
- **Venue:** DIGI LAB, Southeast Asia Center, Tsinghua University
- **Format:** In person
- **Description:** Discusses human–computer interaction's position as an interdisciplinary field and its current development across industry, academia, and research.
- **Panelists:**
- Zhiyong Fu — Associate professor, Academy of Arts and Design, Tsinghua University; president of International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction
- Jun Hu — Associate professor, Eindhoven University of Technology
- Huawei Tu — Assistant professor, La Trobe University
- Yun Wang — Associate professor, Beihang University
- Fang Liu — PhD student, Xiamen University
- **Moderator:** Yingying She
- **Topics:**
1. Innovation through links with other disciplines: Do our ways of thinking as human–computer interaction researchers and designers constrain other possibilities? What should human–computer interaction learn from interdisciplinary exchange, and how can it connect with traditional culture?
1. Industry–academia–research priorities for human–computer interaction: How can university talent development connect seamlessly with companies' innovation needs?
1. Interdisciplinary talent development in human–computer interaction and different models in China and abroad: How can young scholars identify their own roles and collaborators during their education and research careers?
1. Practical directions and important current issues, among other topics.

## Philosophical Foundations and Future Prospects of human–computer interaction

- **Time:** November 13, 2023, 01:00PM–05:30PM
- **Venue:** DIGI LAB, Southeast Asia Center, Tsinghua University
- **Format:** In person
- **Description:** Explores the nature of human–computer interaction thought and its future role.
- **Panelists:**
- Xiangshi Ren — Professor, Kochi University of Technology, Japan; founding president of International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (online)
- Shengdong Zhao — Associate professor, National University of Singapore
- Xiaojuan Ma — Associate professor, The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology
- Pengcheng An — Assistant professor, Southern University of Science and Technology
- **Moderator:** Chen Wang
- **Topics:**
1. Future human–computer relations: Beginning with the panelists' current concerns, we examine how their understanding of interaction developed through their research careers, then discuss human–computer relationships and possible evolution in future interaction paradigms.
1. human–computer interaction and social issues: Technological development affects the psychological, social, and economic lives of individuals and groups, positively and negatively. Should human–computer interaction help explain and address these issues, and how? How does it affect the development of human capabilities?
1. Philosophical foundations of human–computer interaction: Drawing on panelists' diverse backgrounds, how do thought and culture shape the human–computer interaction framework and its values? What should the essential contribution of human–computer interaction be?
1. Directions for reflection: Building on these discussions, we explore practical and collaborative directions, potentially including innovations in human–computer interaction theory, empirical research, and applications.
