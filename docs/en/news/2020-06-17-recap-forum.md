## Recap: ICACHI Online Forum, Session 3

Session 3 of the ICACHI Online Forum was simulcast by Tencent Wiz and XuetangX on Saturday, June 13, Beijing Time. Organized by International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI), the Human-Engaged Computing and Technology for Good series showcases Chinese scholars' shared commitment to combating the pandemic through academic work. As society and the economy slowly restart after the global pandemic's pause, it confronts future challenges and reconsiders technology's social role. It seeks more forward-looking, resilient, and inclusive human–computer interaction models, solutions for known and unknown problems, reflection on progress, and stronger crisis response.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-02.png)

The session's theme was Interaction Technologies for the Future Internet of Things. Dr. Wei Li of Huawei's human–computer interaction Laboratory moderated, with Dr. Huawei Tu from La Trobe University, Australia; Dr. Da-Yuan Huang from Huawei's human–computer interaction Laboratory; and Professor Xing-Dong Yang from Dartmouth College, USA. They discussed new sensing and interaction technologies. Multimodal approaches, AI, AR, and VR may soon enable smart devices to perceive their own and users' spatial environments. human–computer interaction advances in IoT will continue driving technological transformation and IT industry development.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-03.jpg)

## Guest Research Presentations

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-04.png)

### Wei Li

Huawei human–computer interaction Laboratory

## Presentation Topic

Future UI for the Internet of Things

Dr. Wei Li reviewed the history of human–computer interaction. Early human–computer interaction methods were limited, whereas today's expanding range of devices may outgrow earlier interaction approaches. Diverse IoT devices are particularly difficult to control through one universal method. Interaction in the IoT era has three characteristics: multimodality, cross-device operation, and greater naturalness. Rapid Internet and 5G development will move more interaction services to the cloud, integrating clients, connections, and cloud services.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-05.jpg)

However, connecting future smart furniture through current interaction technologies presents five major challenges:

1. Complexity: more smart devices make interaction tasks and learning multi-device systems more complex.
1. Connectivity: devices from different manufacturers struggle to interoperate.
1. Interface consistency: different devices support different interaction methods.
1. Continuity: use across places, times, and devices should remain continuous and uninterrupted.
1. Context awareness: smart devices need to perceive space and time.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-06.jpg)

Dr. Wei Li highlighted two technologies addressing these challenges: gesture control through bodily movement, and spatial computing to improve devices' awareness of space. Future devices should understand users better, with personalized, emotionally responsive interfaces. Dr. Wei Li also identified three development dimensions: multimodal interaction experiences, big data processing, and capabilities for sensing users.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-07.png)

Finally, Dr. Wei Li illustrated current research and applications through examples such as low-fatigue, elbow-supported gesture interaction informed by human factors.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-08.jpg)

### Huawei Tu

La Trobe University, Melbourne, Australia

## Presentation Topic

human–computer interaction Design Based on the Crossing Paradigm

Dr. Huawei Tu began with ideas presented by human–computer interaction pioneer Stuart Card in his invited CHI 2012 lecture:

- “We are in a new golden age for HCI.”
- “New I/O devices are needed.”
- “New major interaction paradigms are possible.”

Gartner's 2018 technology analysis suggested development horizons of five to ten years, or even longer, for human–computer interaction-related areas including AR, VR, smart textiles, and IoT platforms. Amid this rapid development, new input/output devices are needed to meet users' interaction needs, alongside new paradigms to define and support human–computer interaction's advancement.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-10.jpg)

Dr. Huawei Tu discussed input paradigms for different tasks, including pointing, crossing, steering, and gesturing, focusing on crossing. In this important human–computer interaction paradigm, users move a pen, mouse, finger, or another input tool across a target.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-11.gif)

Crossing is a visually and spatially dependent gestural interface. Unlike conventional gesture interfaces, its meaning depends on the targets crossed rather than the movement's shape. It offers three major advantages over other input paradigms:

1. Crossing can perform as well as or better than pointing, making it a potential alternative.
1. Compared with point-and-click, crossing offers richer expressiveness and more varied interaction semantics.
1. Crossing can select multiple commands in one stroke, enabling fluid multicommand interaction.
1. It can combine with paradigms such as clicking and dragging to enrich interaction.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-12.png)

Finally, Dr. Huawei Tu discussed crossing in VR, including selecting distant small targets, sequential targets, and moving targets.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-13.jpg)

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-14.png)

### Da-Yuan Huang

Huawei human–computer interaction Laboratory

## Presentation Topic

Enhancing Interfaces Through Tactile Perception and Feedback

Dr. Da-Yuan Huang considered gaps in interaction with new device forms: why can introducing a novel interface reduce users' willingness to use it? Current devices mainly rely on finger touch, without effectively transferring the capabilities of conventional mice and keyboards.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-15.png)

To bring mouse and keyboard capabilities into finger interaction, Dr. Huang presented Touch Sense and Digit Space. Touch Sense maps different areas of the index finger's skin to left, middle, and right mouse buttons. Digit Space uses sensor arrays on the index and ring fingers and a strong magnet to locate the thumb on the hand, supporting a rich range of gesture inputs.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-16.jpg)

Dr. Huang argued that new interfaces should be strengthened by testing users' perceptual limits. He also shared recent research on AR/VR wearable interaction, including skin-displacement feedback. Examples were the Masque head-mounted display and Gaiters lower-leg device, which stretch skin in different directions and at different intensities to create immersive gaming experiences. He concluded with new feedback forms such as the Glissade stylus, which changes its weight distribution by repositioning a copper tube.

### Xing-Dong Yang

Dartmouth College, USA

## Presentation Topic

Building Interaction Systems for Smart Objects

Professor Xing-Dong Yang discussed smart objects: anything endowed with computation and intelligence, including clothing, furniture, and our own bodies. He envisioned a near future in which computing becomes accessible through varied everyday objects. An example was a denim jacket developed by Wei Li and Google, with cuff sensors supporting simple actions such as hanging up a phone in one's pocket.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-19.gif)

Professor Yang identified three advantages of interaction with smart objects:

1. Unobtrusiveness: smart objects support more natural interaction with fewer interruptions to users' ongoing behavior.
1. Decoupling: interaction controls can be separated from the computing device. For example, a camera can be placed at the best angle while a separate controller supports the photograph.
1. Contextualization: designing smart-object interaction around real contexts enriches experiences within those settings.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-20.png)

Many challenges remain in designing and applying smart objects. Professor Yang examined three aspects of building their interaction systems:

1. Hardware: conventional interactive devices tend to be rectangular, whereas smart objects may be flat, curved, or flexible. The limited development of flexible sensing remains a major challenge.
1. Software: smart objects may not offer a large, comfortable input surface like a phone. Designing comfortable, intuitive interfaces on small devices is another challenge.
1. Energy: as diverse smart objects proliferate in multi-device environments, convenient ways for users to recharge them are still needed.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-17-recap-forum-21.jpg)

To address these challenges, Professor Yang presented:

1. Tessutivo textile sensing, enabling tablecloths, sofas, and similar objects to recognize everyday items that commonly touch them.
1. A QWERTY keyboard on the index fingertip, designed for touch-typing English with the thumb.
1. A self-powered touchpad for small objects, using solar cells to supply energy and recognize gesture input.
