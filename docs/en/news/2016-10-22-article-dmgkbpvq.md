Today, October 21, IxAP 2016—Interactive Technology and Ageing Populations—opened in the auditorium of Kochi University of Technology, Japan. More than 120 researchers, scholars, and industry representatives from 17 countries and regions came together across human–computer interaction, information technology, sociology, and related fields to advance human–computer interaction technologies for older adults. The program includes 12 flash talks, 16 full papers, and 36 posters. The conference is organized by the CHEC laboratory at Kochi University of Technology, led by human–computer interaction expert Professor Xiangshi Ren, in close cooperation with ACM, SIGCHI, and ICACHI.

IxAP formally opened around 9 a.m. with remarks by co-chairs Professors Xiangshi Ren and Antti Oulasvirta. Kochi University of Technology President Masahiko Isobe and School of Information head Hiroaki Shimizu offered welcomes.

Professor Frank Vetere of the University of Melbourne delivered the opening keynote, The Problems of Ageing and What Technology Can Do About It, exploring aging-related challenges and technology's possible contributions.

Many other distinguished professors attended from around the world; we cannot list them all here.

After a short coffee break, paper presentations began. Sixteen papers were accepted, with selected contributions to be recommended to EI-indexed journals.

Morning presentations were followed by lunch and the poster session. Jilin University and Chen Wang ultimately won the judges' recognition and the Best Poster Award. Senior ICACHI members also held in-depth discussions over lunch.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2016-10-22-article-dmgkbpvq-13.jpg)

Professor Joanna McGrenere of the University of British Columbia's Department of Computer Science delivered the closing keynote, Designing a Self-Administered Cognitive Test That Runs Through a Web Browser.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2016-10-22-article-dmgkbpvq-14.jpg)

Before closing, the chair announced that IxAP 2018 would take place in Beijing in October or November 2018, hosted by Beijing Institute of Technology, and encouraged continued attention to interaction technologies for older adults.

There was an unexpected interruption: a magnitude 6.6 earthquake in Tottori produced intensity-3 shaking in Kochi. Fortunately, it did not diminish participants' enthusiasm. Tomorrow, they will explore Kochi's natural scenery and enjoy its autumn landscape.
