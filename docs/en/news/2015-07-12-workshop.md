July 7, 2015

Author: Shiwei Cheng, Zhejiang University of Technology

During ACM CHI 2015, Tsinghua University, the Institute of Software at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Zhejiang University of Technology, and Carnegie Mellon University jointly organized Developing HCI Education Crossing Asia. The workshop discussed human–computer interaction education in Asia-Pacific countries and regions, aiming to introduce human–computer interaction fundamentals to teachers and students, guide human–computer interaction program and curriculum development, improve human–computer interaction education, and train more professionals for universities and industry. It received 11 position papers from Asia, Oceania, and Europe, covering the interdisciplinary foundations and curricula of human–computer interaction, the history of human–computer interaction, and human–computer interaction teaching and course examples.

The one-day workshop attracted nearly 30 participants and featured two invited keynotes.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2015-07-12-workshop-02.jpg)

Figure 1. Professor Henry B.L. Duh delivering a keynote

Professor Henry B.L. Duh (Henry Duh) of the University of Tasmania, Australia, presented Development and History of HCI Education in Asia Pacific, sharing his teaching experiences in the United States, Singapore, Australia, and Taiwan. He argued that the interdisciplinary nature of human–computer interaction enables students of human–computer interaction to achieve richer outcomes, drawing on his experience in human–computer interaction education. He encouraged students from different backgrounds to study human–computer interaction and take varied courses to develop creativity and practical skills.

Professor Gahgene Gweon of KAIST presented Teaching an HCI Course: Tips on Encouraging Interdisciplinary Work During Class. Drawing on her teaching experience, she emphasized that project-based human–computer interaction teaching and assessment can significantly improve practical skills, teamwork, and communication.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2015-07-12-workshop-03.jpg)

Figure 2. Professor Gweon delivering a keynote

Authors of six papers also gave oral presentations. **For example, Dr. Evangelos Kapros of University College Dublin investigated human–computer interaction courses in computer science programs worldwide and found that many universities gave insufficient attention to human–computer interaction. Even in Europe and North America, where human–computer interaction education is most developed, only about 13% of the surveyed programs required human–computer interaction as a core course, while 51% offered it only as an elective. This demonstrates the urgency and necessity of human–computer interaction education, particularly in Asian countries and regions starting from a less developed base.**

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2015-07-12-workshop-04.jpg)

Figure 3. Dr. **Kapros** giving an oral presentation

Participants responded enthusiastically, discussing the presentations in depth. Experts answered questions about improving human–computer interaction curricula and training systems in China, Korea, and other Asian countries. The workshop met its objectives and received consistently positive feedback. Participants advocated extending human–computer interaction courses to more students in computing, industrial design, and related programs, reflecting the field's interdisciplinary and practical nature. They also recommended learning from established teaching practices and bringing in qualified faculty to improve human–computer interaction education across developing Asian countries. Participants strongly supported continuing the workshop and began discussing the following year's plans.

The workshop organizers were leading human–computer interaction experts: Professor Yuanchun Shi of Tsinghua University, Research Professor Feng Tian of the Institute of Software at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Associate Professor Shiwei Cheng of Zhejiang University of Technology, and Professor Anind Dey of Carnegie Mellon University. The event also received strong support from SIGCHI Executive Committee member Professor Zhengjie Liu of Dalian Maritime University, the human–computer interaction committee of China Computer Federation (CCF), and experts from International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI).
