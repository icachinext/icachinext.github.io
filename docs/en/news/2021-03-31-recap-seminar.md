**IWHEC 2021 took place online through Zoom on March 19–20, 2021.** It focused on rethinking human–computer relations and building a common platform for exchanging ideas worldwide, developing greater collective capability, and creating better living environments. Twelve researchers in human–computer interaction, neuroscience, computer science, AI, engaged humans, engaging technologies, and design presented and discussed with participants worldwide. Open to all through webcasts and real-time streaming, the workshop reached 2,608 people across Zoom registrations and three viewing platforms.

## Conference Recap

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2021-03-31-recap-seminar-04.png)

On the morning of March 19, President Masahiko Isobe of Japan's Kochi University of Technology delivered opening remarks. Professor Xiangshi Ren of Kochi University of Technology introduced Human-Engaged Computing, and Professor Xiaojuan Ma of The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology outlined the program.

Daisuke Sakamoto of Hokkaido University moderated keynote talks and discussion by Yingqing Xu of Tsinghua University's Academy of Arts and Design, Gloria Mark of the University of California, Irvine, and Abhishek Gupta of the Montreal AI Ethics Institute.

Professor Yingqing Xu discussed social responsibility in design innovation, stressing designers' rational reflection on their role bridging humanities and technology, fresh perspectives on uncertainty in AI applications, and insights from his educational work.

Professor Gloria Mark discussed digital media's psychological effects on attention, stress, and productivity, and ways to manage them.

Abhishek Gupta introduced the GTSSD framework applying design and human–computer interaction principles, illustrated its practical use through case studies, and considered integration into existing workflows to support widespread, relatively accessible adoption.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2021-03-31-recap-seminar-05.png)

Professor Xiangmin Fan of Institute of Software, Chinese Academy of Sciences moderated the afternoon session on March 19. Keynotes and discussion featured Anirudha Joshi of IIT's IDC School of Design, Fang You of Tongji University's School of Arts and Communication, and Gilbert Cockton of Northumbria University's School of Design.

Professor Anirudha Joshi presented a user adoption model describing how emergent users adapt to information and communication technologies, design strategies supporting adoption, and remaining challenges.

Professor Fang You shared research on guidelines for Chinese character size and text length on intelligent vehicle center-console interfaces.

Professor Gilbert Cockton examined how Human-Engaged Computing can create valuable interactions through design. He stressed considering design, evaluation, purpose, beneficiaries, and when and how people benefit—including those who do not. He argued for extending connections across human–computer interaction while advancing and connecting design arenas in parallel, and introduced value sketches and maps as two supporting methods.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2021-03-31-recap-seminar-06.png)

On the morning of March 20 Beijing Time, Professor Xiaojun Yuan of the University at Albany, SUNY, opened the session and introduced a planned international journal on Human-Engaged Computing to advance related Human-Engaged Computing research.

Professor Geehyuk Lee of KAIST moderated keynotes and discussion by Microsoft researcher Jonathan Grudin, Professor Hong Z. Tan of Purdue University's electrical and computer engineering department, and Assistant Professor Jian Zhao of the University of Waterloo's Cheriton School of Computer Science.

Jonathan Grudin described research identifying patterns in thousands of people's conversations with a virtual entity over several years and an architecture intended to reduce risks and encourage friendlier dialogue. Examples of Internet-amplified harmful information illustrated the need for and challenges of Human-Engaged Computing.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2021-03-31-recap-seminar-07.png)

Professor Hong Z. Tan first presented research on natural tactile languages used by people with severe hearing or vision impairments, then discussed projects conveying emotion and art through touch. She explored how haptic interfaces can communicate calmly and pleasantly to advance Human-Engaged Computing research.

Dr. Jian Zhao discussed understanding and using predictive models with visualization to keep humans in the loop. Examples covered real-world data exploration, online education, and network analysis, alongside future directions for using visualization to improve general data analysis workflows.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2021-03-31-recap-seminar-08.png)

On the afternoon of March 20, Dr. Sayan Sarcar of the University of Tsukuba moderated keynotes and discussion by Dr. Miwako Doi of Japan's NICT, Dr. Kazuhisa Shibata of RIKEN's Human Cognition and Learning Laboratory, and Dr. Johanna Schmidt of Austria's VRVis Center for Visual Computing.

Miwako Doi discussed how ICT crosses national, gender, physical and control-space boundaries, physical limitations, and proprietary rights to build a data-driven society. Human–machine interfaces therefore involve interaction between people and that society and among people within it. Yet data-driven society creates new boundaries, including ethnic and civic nationalism and cyberattacks. COVID-19 has also accelerated the contactless economy. Humans and data-driven society must overcome these new boundaries.

Kazuhisa Shibata explained how decoded neurofeedback (DecNef) induces specific activation patterns in targeted brain regions to change behaviors and conditions. Its potential as a medical intervention and a bidirectionally adaptive brain–computer system offers new opportunities to rethink human–computer relations.

Johanna Schmidt reviewed established concepts of data visualization literacy and important directions for future work.

ICACHI President Zhiyong Fu described the association's commitment to cross-cultural cooperation and sharing through human–computer synergy, linking technological innovation with the future. Executive Vice President Xiaojuan Ma then thanked speakers, staff, and participants. A group photograph marked the workshop's conclusion.
