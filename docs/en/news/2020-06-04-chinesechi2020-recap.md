The eighth International Chinese human–computer interaction Conference, Chinese CHI, was held online on May 30, 2020, simulcast by Tencent Wiz. Organized by International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction (ICACHI), the conference has become an important connection among Chinese HCI scholars, academia, and industry worldwide. Since 2012, seven successful editions have taken place in Paris, Toronto, Seoul, Silicon Valley, Guangzhou, Montreal, and Xiamen.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-04-chinesechi2020-recap-02.png)

The conference opened with remarks from Chinese CHI chair Junfeng Yao of Xiamen University. Against China's emphasis on cultural confidence, cultural strength, and research grounded in its own society, human–computer interaction offers an interdisciplinary platform where talent and ingenuity can flourish.

The seventh Chinese CHI in Xiamen reached an unprecedented scale through participation across professions and fields. Although special circumstances required the eighth Chinese CHI to move online, participants' enthusiasm remained palpable even without seeing their faces. At the opening, Junfeng Yao wished Chinese CHI 2020 every success and sincerely thanked organizers, participants, and volunteers for their contributions.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-04-chinesechi2020-recap-03.png)

Wei Li, president of International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction, then addressed the conference, briefly introducing International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction and wishing Chinese CHI 2020 success. International Chinese Association of Computer Human Interaction promotes solidarity and cooperation in the international scientific community through an open platform connecting scientists in China and overseas. Known as ICACHI, it was formally founded in Austin, Texas, in May 2012 and held its first successful Chinese CHI forum in France the following year. In 2014, the association registered in Toronto as a nonprofit professional organization and held its second conference there. Its proceedings were included in the ACM Digital Library, with papers discoverable through major research indexes.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-04-chinesechi2020-recap-04.jpg)

2020 was destined to be an extraordinary year. The association introduced Human-Engaged Computing and Technology for Good, calling for joint use of science and technology to combat the pandemic and protect humanity's shared home. It launched an online forum series under this theme, with Chinese CHI 2020 joining as part of the technology-enabled pandemic response.

As society and the global economy slowly restart after the pandemic's pause, the Human-Engaged Computing and Technology for Good forums seek to confront humanity's future challenges and reconsider technology's social role. The association hopes to envision more forward-looking and inclusive human–computer interaction models, develop applications and solutions to unknown problems, inspire reflection on progress, and strengthen crisis response.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-04-chinesechi2020-recap-05.png)

## Keynote

Chinese CHI invited Guozhong Dai and Ligang Liu as keynote speakers.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-04-chinesechi2020-recap-06.jpg)

### Guozhong Dai

Researcher and doctoral supervisor at Institute of Software, Chinese Academy of Sciences; chief scientist of the human–computer interaction Technology and Intelligent Information Processing Laboratory.

Guozhong Dai presented Fifty Years of Reflection: What Exactly Is human–computer interaction? Drawing on five decades of research, Guozhong Dai discussed human–computer interaction (Computer-Human Interaction) through epistemological and methodological perspectives and examined current shortcomings and challenges in human–computer interaction. Guozhong Dai also introduced strategic thinking about IT and the PBUI concept: screens encountered upon opening one's eyes, with multiple screens per person.

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-04-chinesechi2020-recap-07.png)

![Article image](https://media.icachi.org/news/2020-06-04-chinesechi2020-recap-08.png)

### Ligang Liu

Professor, University of Science and Technology of China

Ligang Liu presented Walking Through Large Virtual Environments. Ligang Liu first introduced virtual reality: narrowly understood as stereoscopic visual presentation, it uses equipment to engage or deceive senses including vision, hearing, smell, touch, and taste, creating entirely virtual worlds for experience and interaction. Ligang Liu then examined walking and navigation challenges in VR—limited physical space, complex multiuser interaction, and difficult gait detection—alongside current technical solutions, and discussed practical applications extending from virtual to augmented reality.
