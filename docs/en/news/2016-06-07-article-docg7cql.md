The Center for Human-Engaged Computing (CHEC) at Kochi University of Technology is an internationally renowned, forward-looking institute in human–computer interaction. It is led by Professor Xiangshi Ren, lifetime honorary president of ICACHI.

Professor Xiangshi Ren began fundamental and applied human–computer interaction research in 1991. He established the human–computer interaction Technology Laboratory (Ren Lab) at Kochi University of Technology in 2000 and the Center for Human-Computer Interaction, a human–computer interaction institute, in 2012. Building on this work, he proposed Human-Engaged Computing (HEC) in 2013 and founded CHEC in 2015.

Ren Lab group photograph

**CHEC's research extends beyond conventional HCI, combining software implementation and hardware development with psychology, cognitive science, neuroscience, psychiatry, human–robot interaction, and sensing. These perspectives deepen understanding of HCI and HEC. CHEC collaborates closely with research organizations and universities including Microsoft Research, Google, the University of Toronto, Aalto University, and The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology.**

**CHEC focuses on agency in human–computer interaction to enhance human intelligence and capabilities. Its members come from many countries and cultures, including Japan, China, Australia, Thailand, Korea, India, Myanmar, Indonesia, Iran, Egypt, and Finland. They bring cultural diversity and creativity, building bridges between East and West.**

## 1 Research Achievements

Two papers published in ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction, a leading human–computer interaction journal, in 2000 and 2015—*Improving Selection Performance on Pen-Based Systems: A Study of Pen-Based Interaction for Selection Tasks* and *Differences and Similarities Between Finger and Pen Stroke Gestures on Stationary and Mobile Devices*—have advanced pen and touch interaction research on stationary and mobile devices.

The institute has published more than 280 papers in major journals and international conferences, including 70 journal papers and 146 peer-reviewed conference papers. More than 110 are indexed by SCI, EI, or ISTP. Publications include leading human–computer interaction journals such as ACM TOCHI, IJHCS, IJHCI, and BIT, and conferences including ACM CHI, UIST, ITS, DIS, and IFIP INTERACT.

## 2 Student Development

The institute follows world-leading directions in human–computer interaction while identifying future directions in human–computer interaction and conducting pioneering research. Formal laboratory rules and seminars coexist with varied projects and respect for students' interests. Leading faculty develop ambitious ideas, while rigorous postdoctoral researchers provide comprehensive training that helps members pursue their aspirations. The institute also offers **many opportunities for external exchange**: **visits to other laboratories and talks by distinguished guests broaden students' perspectives.**

**Professor Xiangshi Ren has supervised 11 doctoral graduates who now actively research human–computer interaction as professors or researchers worldwide. Some stayed as assistant professors; others undertook postdoctoral work at Swansea University, RIKEN, Nagoya University, or the University of Tokyo, or returned home to faculty positions.**

## 3 Research Projects

Current projects include:

- Interfaces for older adults, a Japan–Finland JST collaboration
- The relationship between exergames and Alzheimer's disease
- fMRI and mid-air sensing interaction devices
- Electrovibration through pen devices to investigate interfaces for blind people
- The effects of games on brain structure

## 4 Life Beyond Research

**To improve collaboration, quality of life, and members' health, CHEC organizes open philosophical discussions and group activities, including hiking, visiting parks and galleries, seaside walks, and meditation. Members enjoy nature, the excitement of reaching summits, and views of the ocean. Annual welcome and farewell parties feature food from different cultures. Learning and enjoyment go together, helping members find inspiration, restore balance, and flourish within this extended family.**

CHEC members come from around the world. Seminars and everyday conversations can be conducted in English, giving everyone opportunities to improve their language skills.
