## An ICACHI Reflection

Stepping off the plane into June temperatures above 30 degrees, I was back in Guangzhou's familiar sauna-like weather. Seven months at Kochi University of Technology felt both long and short: a city had gone from unfamiliar to familiar, and now I had left it.

November 2, 2015: an overcast day in Kochi. The journey from the airport to KUT passed through countryside so green that I could almost smell the grass from inside the car. Japanese houses stood close to the road, with low hills beyond. Everything was peaceful and deeply comforting. My first impression was of a setting from Doraemon.

The campus was equally quiet, even though it was a working day. Welcoming staff carefully explained rules and everyday customs to us newcomers, while Japanese students helped us settle in. They were shy when we first met and remained so afterward. In the laboratory, most members greeted us warmly; that friendliness continued throughout the seven months.

Over seven months, I experienced winter, spring, and summer. The sky was always blue, hills filled my dormitory window, and the sunset differed from those in southern China. Aircraft traced lines through orange skies while cattle and sheep grazed on the hills. I often watched until darkness fell. The brilliant star-filled skies I remembered from childhood appeared on every clear night. Walking back from the laboratory, I would stop to gaze upward before returning to my room.

CHEC was among KUT's largest laboratories, with twenty to thirty members ranging from postdoctoral researchers to undergraduates. We came from different countries and spoke English with different accents, but shared a goal: to be good students and colleagues, conduct research, make breakthroughs, and grow. There was no rigid hierarchy; anyone facing a difficulty could count on others setting aside their own work to help. That is teamwork. I was fortunate to join the large JST * project, Information Systems for Accessibility and Support of Older People, [http://www.jst.go.jp/pr/info/info1094/index_e.html](http://www.jst.go.jp/pr/info/info1094/index_e.html)）* soon after arriving. Although I handled only the experimental implementation, I learned a great deal.

We were an active group, frequently organizing outings and parties. I thank Professor Ren for letting me organize the annual laboratory party, which taught me about planning and management. Who says a science and engineering laboratory must be monotonous? We enjoyed life: hiking along the coast, playing in the waves, discussing philosophy beneath cherry blossoms, and lingering in Monet's Garden in late spring *(http://www.kjmonet.jp/english.html)*.

After returning home, I missed everything: CHEC's welcoming members, cooking unfamiliar dishes, authentic Japanese food, and the laboratory's everyday moments. May CHEC continue to flourish, and may my friends achieve their academic and professional aspirations. I am Wen Dongcai, a small part of CHEC.
